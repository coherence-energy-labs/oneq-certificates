I went through all three in detail. I recomputed the headline arithmetic, re-derived each soundness lemma, cross-checked numbers between the papers, and confirmed that the 2026 references exist.

Overall verdict: I found no soundness errors. Every lemma I re-derived holds:

the safe-dual bound and lattice rule (A)
the θ-scaled T-cut packing bound (B)
the projection lemma, affine-observable anchoring, packing and cover bounds, and the contiguous-window argument (C)

Almost every count I recomputed reconciles, including table sums, Clopper–Pearson bounds and McNemar p-values. The problems are elsewhere: what the headline numbers actually measure, a few internal contradictions, and evidence weaker than its framing. C is the strongest paper. A has the biggest framing problem. B has the biggest strategic exposure.

Paper A: BP-OSD receipts on [[144,12,12]]

Must fix

The “instrument” headline measures a misconfigured baseline.
The abstract leads with BP-OSD missing the optimum on 178/300 stress syndromes. You then show this is mostly unscaled min-sum, and scaled BP-OSD misses 1.
A referee will read the preregistered comparison as a strawman that was rescued post hoc.
The fix is cheap, because the stored proofs already fix the optimum on every shot. Re-decode all 8 datasets with scaling 0.625 as the primary configuration and score optimum-hits against the existing receipts.
You skipped re-decoding the heterogeneous, phenomenological and circuit datasets, and nothing prevents it. That leaves “this attribution does not extend to the 32” as an avoidable hole.
Ideally, preregister the scaled configuration on fresh seeds. Then demote 178/300 out of the abstract.
Tier 1 has two definitions.
Sec. III defines tier 1 as any flat dual meeting L > U − 1/D on BP-OSD’s answer.
A single-node “tree” is exactly such a dual. In Sec. VI D you admit the producer issued tier 1 only on integral-primal shots, so the tier-1 column of Table III is not Sec. III’s tier 1.
The same issue may affect the held-out campaign. If any of the 3 tree shots where BP-OSD attains the optimum were single-dual trees, they are tier 1 under your own definition.
Tiers currently depend on the producer’s path, not on what the receipt proves. Pick one definition and recount.
The datasets are undefined.
“Uniform”, “X-sector” and “stress” are given only by p. I can’t tell which check matrix each uses, how many columns, or what distinguishes uniform p=0.02 from X-sector p=0.02.
The trivial-shot rates (98/398 at p=0.01, 23/493 at p=0.02) are consistent with a 144-column single-sector problem. That makes the difference from “X-sector” even less clear.
State H, the column count and the fault model per dataset.

Should fix

The circuit noise model conflicts with Paper C.
A’s circuit dataset does not fault idle locations. C’s IBM placement does fault them, and C establishes d_circ = 10 under that placement.
A cites C for “the circuit distance of this cycle is 10”. Under A’s noise family, containment gives only ≥ 10.
C’s [[72,12,6]] test suggests the mechanism sets coincide, but nobody checked. Compare A’s 2,304-mechanism projected model against C’s T=3 model and state the result.
Name what the receipts add over a trusted MIP solve. “Measures a decoder without the true error” is also what running HiGHS to optimality gives you. The receipt contributes trust in that optimum, not the capability. Say so, or a referee will.
The scale-factor check is off by one. The check “certified 299/299/299” doesn’t say which dataset was used, or why it isn’t 300.
“Independent” implementations are all yours.
The JavaScript checkers were written “with AI assistance and without consulting either Python file.” That is self-attested, and the AI’s context isn’t logged.
Either log the session context, or stop using independence language for these checkers.

Minor: The 1,851 development flat certificates were “re-verified” but not stored, so that claim can’t be replayed; drop it or mark it. Cite the exact ldpc 2.4.1 source lines for both scaling defaults. I couldn’t confirm the BpOsdDecoder default from the docs, and the docs example for the Sinter wrapper passes 0.625 explicitly, which doesn’t establish its default.

Paper B: Matching certificates

Must fix

The second ε-verifier contradicts your own argument.
Sec. III argues that a same-author, same-language checker “cannot expose a shared misreading,” and that is why you wrote a JavaScript checker.
But every headline rate’s second verdict comes from a scaled-integer Python verifier, which is exactly that kind of checker.
The JavaScript checker implements only the exact checker, not the ε-checker.
The T-oddness defect is the evidence this matters: the JavaScript checker always refused those forgeries, but no differential test ever compared the two implementations.
Port the ε semantics to JavaScript and run differential fuzzing between the two on random and adversarial inputs.
The held-out test excludes the configuration most likely to be overfit.
Generations 5 and 6 were developed only on the d=7, 30-round shots. Those shots are 50% of the hardware total, and their 99.998% figure is in the abstract.
The held-out test excludes d=7 “for cost,” so that figure has no out-of-sample check at all. Run even 2,000 held-out d=7 shots.
The Sec. VIII BP-OSD gap results are likely the same artifact as Paper A.
No BP-OSD configuration is stated, and gaps up to 148 weight units look like unscaled min-sum.
The section also contradicts itself. It says BP-OSD decodes the graphlike components of the decomposed model, then explains the gaps by BP-OSD “maximising likelihood over the full hypergraph.” Only one of these can be true.
Restricting to exact-checker-certified shots (253/400 at d=7) introduces selection bias toward easy shots. ε-certification at 10⁻⁶ resolves O(1) weight gaps fine, so use it instead.
Cut or redo Sec. VI E. Its certificates were not kept, not replayed and not attacked, against the paper’s own standard. Its scientific content is nil by your own Lemma: certificates don’t care where the weights came from. The closing novelty paragraph then claims a combination for witnesses you didn’t keep.

Strategic point a referee may raise

Sparse Blossom is itself a primal–dual algorithm. Its region radii form a laminar packing of T-odd sets on the detector graph, which is exactly your certificate object. If PyMatching exported them, you would likely get near-100% coverage at roughly decode cost, which would make the LP ladder and the “3,200× / offline-only” conclusion look like artifacts of the producer.

The caveat is that PyMatching’s integer weight discretization bounds the slack. That bound may exceed 10⁻⁶ and would force revisiting ε_max.
Your generation-3 rung already translates blossom duals, so also explain why that translation doesn’t close everything.
Either explain why exporting the decoder’s own dual doesn’t work, or do it.

Should fix

Tables II and III round differently. For d=3, 30 rounds, Z: 99.99 vs 99.98. For d=5, 13 rounds, Z: 99.72 vs 99.71. Table II rounds to nearest and Table III floors. Unify.
The seeded-case corpus is off by one. “4,924 seeded cases” ≠ 4,861 defective + 62 alternative optima = 4,923.
Confidence-interval conventions are mixed. Sec. IV uses one-sided 95% bounds. Sec. VII uses two-sided Clopper–Pearson upper bounds (0.478%, 5.78%). State the convention.
Explain or drop the forgery class used once. The “strictly heavier valid correction” class applied to 1 of 10⁶ shots. Generic forgeries are mostly refused by the syndrome check alone.
The 8M-forgery battery never contained the one defect that actually existed; an LP search on small graphs found it.
Promote that adversary to a standing test over the replay corpus, and drop “every forged certificate was refused” from the abstract.
The stale-calibration experiment is weakly motivated. If you have the reference model, diffing the two models detects the drift without certificates. State what certificates add beyond that, such as per-shot localization.

Minor: Replace Table V, which still uses the deprecated tolerance-rule data, with the rerun numbers. “430× a batched decode” compares a median check time to a mean decode time.

Paper C: Circuit-distance certificates

This is the cleanest of the three. The evidence classes are honest and the Lean calculus is a real contribution.

Should fix

The every-T claims rest on hand proofs reviewed only by an AI agent.
Theorems 1 and 2 were “AI tools assisted with … the proofs,” audited once by an AI agent. That is the weakest link under the Table I headline.
Theorem 2 is explicit linear algebra over GF(2) and looks formalizable. Formalize it, get one human expert to read both proofs, or label the every-T results as conditional in the abstract.
Exchange circuit files with Pan and Crocker. You “did not exchange circuit files” with either. Running your checker on Pan’s three-colour circuit turns “agreement in each work’s own transcription” into agreement on one object. That costs an email.
The gross-code trust chain still runs through C.
The abstract wording, “checker whose proof calculus is proven sound in Lean,” is accurate. Readers will still hear “Lean-verified gross-code distances,” and only 4 tiny models are kernel-checked.
An intermediate step that removes the C-to-Lean correspondence gap at gross-code scale is an executable checker written in Lean and compiled, which trusts the Lean compiler but not your C.
The proof-regeneration paragraph in Sec. III F is confusing.
It uses two different “30"s: the 30 proofs that don’t ship, and the 30 of 282 regenerated proofs that differ.
It also says “each of the 30 … has thus been produced twice” after reporting only 18 regenerations at other thread counts.
Replace the paragraph with a small table: proof set, count, shipped, regenerated, byte-identical.

Minor:

Tree circuits are only machine-checked to T=6; Table II’s “kernel” column for t30a could be read as more than that.
Cite Shaw–Terhal 2604.09797 as v2; it was revised 20 Apr to correct an error in Sec. 6.3.2. That’s not the appendix you cite, but pin the version.
Strikis et al.’s Appendix H says that no gross-code circuit with circuit distance above 10 had been found, and that only three tilings survive with an upper bound of 11. This matches your “11 (conj.)” label.
Across all three papers
Readability is the largest non-technical risk. The main texts carry audit-log narrative inline: the stdout-drain bug, exit codes, regeneration byte counts. Many sentences stack three or four qualifiers. Move provenance into a “verification log” appendix per paper, and let the main text state each claim once with its evidence class. Referees will otherwise conclude there is more process than result.
Every independence claim bottoms out in you plus AI. The disclosures are honest. The single highest-value step for all three papers is one outside person running the one-command replay and reporting back.

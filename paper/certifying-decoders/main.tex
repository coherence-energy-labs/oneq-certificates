\title{Certifying quantum error-correction decoders: independently checkable near-optimality receipts at scale}

\OwnerAuthor
\OwnerAffiliation

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A quantum error-correction decoder returns a correction and nothing else, and the only property routinely checked, that it explains the syndrome, cannot detect a valid correction heavier than necessary. For minimum-weight decoding, the correction is a minimum-weight $T$-join, whose linear-programming dual is a packing of $T$-odd cuts; any packing, scaled to feasibility, lower-bounds every correction by weak duality, whoever produced it. We build a producer of such packings and a separately written checker that decides in exact rational arithmetic whether a packing proves its correction within $10^{-6}$ of optimal, and keep every receipt. On \EpsSimShots\ simulated shots of a distance-5 surface code over 25 rounds, \EpsSimAcceptedPct\% of corrections were proven within $10^{-6}$ of optimal. On Google Willow shots decoded by PyMatching on the published error models, the base ladder proved \EpsHwGenFourPct\% of \EpsHwGenFourNontrivial\ on ten configurations and the full ladder \EpsHwGenSixPct\% of \EpsHwGenSixNontrivial\ at distance 7 over 30 rounds, shots the producer was developed on. On held-out 50-round runs no generation had read, under a protocol committed beforehand, the frozen base ladder proved all \HoWDThreeN\ distance-3 corrections and \HoWDFivePct\% of \HoWDFiveN\ at distance 5, against \DevDFiveThirteenPct\% and \DevDFiveThirtyPct\% at distance 5 over 13 and 30 rounds in development; distance 7 was excluded in advance. All receipts replay under two separately written verifiers, and every forged certificate tried on them was refused. By weak duality a sound checker refuses every correction heavier than the optimum by more than $10^{-6}$, including faults syndrome checking cannot see; what experiment measures is the false-alarm rate, \DetCleanFalseAlarms\ of \DetCleanN\ unmodified corrections with the base ladder and \DetCleanFalseAlarmsFull\ with the full ladder. At distance 5 over 25 rounds certification takes a median of \CertifyMsMedian~ms (mean \CertifyMsMean~ms), on average about \CostRatioBatch\ times batch decoding, so with current producers it suits offline audit, not the real-time loop.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A decoder is software, and software has defects. An assurance argument for a fault-tolerant memory therefore has to answer a concrete question: if the decoder were subtly wrong, what would notice? In current practice the answer is syndrome consistency. A correction is accepted when the set of detectors it makes odd equals the set that fired, a property every functioning decoder satisfies by construction.

Syndrome consistency is blind to the failure that matters most for a minimum-weight decoder: a correction that explains the syndrome but is not the lightest one. A defective priority queue, a stale cache, or a mis-set tie-break can produce exactly such corrections. They pass every syndrome check and raise the logical error rate, and detecting them by benchmarking means resolving a rate change against a noisy baseline, where decoder defects are confounded with device drift.

Minimum-weight decoding admits a better check. The problem is a minimum-weight $T$-join~\cite{edmondsjohnson1973}, its linear-programming dual is a packing of $T$-odd cuts, and weak duality makes any feasible packing a lower bound on every correction, regardless of the algorithm that produced it. A correction whose weight meets such a bound is optimal, and the packing is a witness that a third party can check in time proportional to its size and the edges it touches, without a solver and without trusting the decoder. This is in the spirit of the certifying algorithms of McConnell \textit{et al.}~\cite{mcconnell2011certifying} and the program checkers of Blum and Kannan~\cite{blumkannan1995}, though not complete in their sense: our producer does not find a witness for every correct output.

This paper reports the engineering and measurement needed to make that observation carry weight in practice:
\begin{itemize}
\item a producer of packings whose receipts prove \EpsSimAcceptedPct\% of simulated corrections, and \EpsHwGenFourPct\% (base ladder) and \EpsHwGenSixPct\% (full ladder) of hardware corrections, within $10^{-6}$ of optimal (Secs.~\ref{sec:producer} and~\ref{sec:results});
\item a checker written independently of the producer, a second verifier in scaled-integer arithmetic that re-decides every receipt, and an adversarial battery whose attacks are themselves audited for falsity (Secs.~\ref{sec:checker}, \ref{sec:semantics} and~\ref{sec:results});
\item a precise account of what a receipt proves when weights are IEEE-754 doubles, a checker that enforces that claim in exact arithmetic, and a complete rerun of the scale experiments under it with every receipt kept (Secs.~\ref{sec:semantics} and~\ref{sec:results});
\item two detection experiments, on seeded decoder faults and on an upstream calibration fault, where syndrome checking detects nothing: their positive arm follows from weak duality, and what they measure is the false-alarm rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:detection}); and
\item per-shot optimality-gap distributions for BP-OSD and belief-matching against certified optima (Sec.~\ref{sec:gaps}).
\end{itemize}
Related work, and what this paper does not claim, are collected in Sec.~\ref{sec:related}.

\section{Certificates for minimum-weight decoding}
\label{sec:certificates}

Let $G=(V,E,w)$ be a decoding graph with non-negative edge weights and a virtual boundary vertex $b$, and let $\T\subseteq V$ be the set of fired detectors, with $b$ added when $|\T|$ is odd so that $|\T|$ is even. A correction is an edge set $J\subseteq E$ whose odd-degree vertices are exactly $\T$, a $\T$-join; minimum-weight decoding asks for one of least weight $w(J)=\sum_{e\in J}w_e$~\cite{dennis2002topological,higgott2025sparse}.

A set $S\subseteq V$ is $\T$-odd when $|S\cap\T|$ is odd, and $\delt(S)$ denotes the edges with exactly one endpoint in $S$. Every $\T$-join meets every $\T$-odd cut, which gives the packing problem
\begin{equation}
\max \sum_S z_S \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{S:\,e\in\delt(S)} z_S \le w_e\ \ \forall e\in E,\qquad z\ge 0,
\label{eq:packing}
\end{equation}
over $\T$-odd sets $S$. For any feasible $z$ and any $\T$-join $J$,
\begin{equation}
w(J) \;\ge\; \sum_{e\in J}\sum_{S:\,e\in\delt(S)} z_S \;=\; \sum_S z_S\,|J\cap\delt(S)| \;\ge\; \sum_S z_S ,
\label{eq:weakduality}
\end{equation}
so a packing whose value equals $w(J)$ proves $J$ minimum. Two properties make this a useful certificate. The argument never refers to how $z$ was obtained, so the checker need not trust the producer. And the constraints are edge-local: feasibility is verified by accumulating, per edge, the values of the sets whose boundary it crosses.

Existence is not in question. Edmonds and Johnson showed that the $\T$-cut constraints describe the dominant of the $\T$-join polytope~\cite{edmondsjohnson1973}, so the linear program~\eqref{eq:packing} attains the minimum $\T$-join weight and an attaining packing exists for every syndrome. What the rates below measure is therefore how often our producer finds one, not whether one exists; Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle} checks the decoder's side independently.

\paragraph*{A dual that looks right and is not.} Node potentials with $y_u+y_v\le w_e$ are the dual of a different primal. They constrain adjacent detectors only, so two detectors joined by a path rather than an edge are unconstrained. In development, run against PyMatching at distance 7, such a ``bound'' exceeded the weight of the correction it was shown. The checker refuses any bound above the primal as impossible, and a certificate built from node potentials is refused outright.

\section{The checker}
\label{sec:checker}

Given the graph, the syndrome, a correction and a packing, the checker (i)~verifies that the correction's odd-degree set equals $\T$; (ii)~verifies that every $z_S$ is non-negative and every $S$ is $\T$-odd, since admitting an even set lets the bound exceed the optimum; (iii)~accumulates the load every edge carries; and (iv)~scales the packing by the largest factor, at most one, that makes every edge constraint hold and compares the scaled value with $w(J)$ in exact arithmetic, reporting the difference as the proven slack (Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}). A value above $w(J)$ is impossible for valid input and is reported as a verifier failure, never accepted as a better bound. Every decoding graph here is simple, with no two edges sharing both endpoints (checked on each persisted instance), so an edge is named by its endpoints and a correction's weight is unambiguous. Loads are accumulated from each set's boundary, so the work is proportional to the total size of the listed sets and of the edges incident to them, not to the graph size times the number of sets; a packing over large sets costs correspondingly more to check. Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling} shows median check time growing with exponent \CheckExponent\ in the number of edges ($R^2=\CheckRSquared$).

A first implementation that tested every edge against every dual was close to quadratic; walking set boundaries removed it.}

\paragraph*{Certificates that travel.} A certificate is useful only if a stranger can re-check it, which requires three properties that are easy to lose. It is bound to its graph by a fingerprint the verifier recomputes from its own copy of the graph; otherwise a genuine certificate for an easy instance can be replayed against a hard one with every arithmetic check passing. Every floating-point value travels as its 64-bit pattern, because decimal round-trips differ across languages. And a shot that does not certify still receives a document, labelled not proven, with its gap, so that failure and silence are distinguishable. A signature attests authorship only: a signature over a false certificate is a signed false certificate.

\paragraph*{A second implementation.} A checker written by the same author in the same language cannot expose a shared misreading of the problem. We wrote a second checker in JavaScript from the specification, sharing no code and depending only on the runtime's built-ins. It implements the specification of the exact-optimality checker, not the $\eps$-checker of Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics} that the results use; the $\eps$-checker's second implementation is the scaled-integer verifier of the replay (Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}), written separately in the same language. The JavaScript checker and the exact checker agree on all \ConfVectors\ conformance vectors, \ConfRefusals\ of which are refusals, since a suite in which everything verifies would not notice a checker that accepts everything; the test suite corrupts one dual value and requires the harness to report the disagreement. Writing the second checker exposed a genuine ambiguity. Given a packing that listed the same vertex set twice with different values, the exact checker deserialised it into a dictionary, kept the last value, and accepted; the JavaScript checker summed the list, found an edge constraint violated, and refused. One document produced two verdicts. A packing is a map from sets to values, and a list encodes one only while its keys are distinct, so both implementations now refuse a repeated set and the case is a permanent conformance vector. A port of the first implementation would have inherited its reading of the ambiguity; this is why the second was written from the specification.

\section{What a receipt proves under floating-point weights}
\label{sec:semantics}

Decoding weights are log-likelihood ratios stored as IEEE-754 doubles, and packings are computed in floating point. Exact equality between a packing's value and $w(J)$ is therefore rarely observable, and a checker must decide what to accept. The choice changes the claim, so we separate two claims:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{exact}: $w(J)=\min_{J'} w(J')$ over the double-valued weights, exactly;
\item \emph{$\eps$-optimal}: $w(J)-\min_{J'} w(J')\le\eps$, with $\eps$ proven.
\end{itemize}
Every double is a rational number, so either claim can be decided in exact arithmetic. The following lemma turns any non-negative packing over $\T$-odd sets, feasible or slightly overloaded, into a proven slack.

\begin{lemma}
Let $z\ge0$ be supported on $\T$-odd sets, let $\ell_e=\sum_{S:\,e\in\delt(S)}z_S$, and let $\theta=\min\bigl(1,\min_{e:\,\ell_e>0} w_e/\ell_e\bigr)$. Then $\theta z$ is feasible for~\eqref{eq:packing}, and for every $\T$-join $J$ with minimum $J^\star$,
\[
w(J)-w(J^\star)\;\le\;\eps(J,z)\;:=\;w(J)-\theta\sum_S z_S .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each constraint of $\theta z$ reads $\theta\ell_e\le w_e$, which holds by the choice of $\theta$. Applying~\eqref{eq:weakduality} to $\theta z$ and $J^\star$ gives $w(J^\star)\ge\theta\sum_S z_S$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph*{The rule the original runs used.} The runs first made for this work, whose rates Sec.~\ref{sec:results} keeps only as a development record, used a checker that accepted a certificate when its floating-point gap was at most $10^{-6}$ and no edge was overloaded by more than $10^{-9}$. An external audit of this work showed that such a rule is not a proof. Any positive tolerance of this kind accepts a correspondingly small suboptimality (a correction of weight $1.0000005$ was accepted against a true optimum of $1$), which the lemma bounds only for admissible packings. Those checkers had no guard against non-finite values, so a NaN in a packing would have been accepted, and they never tested that a packing's sets are $\T$-odd, so they accepted packings over sets that are not cut constraints at all, one of them for a correction of weight 5 against an optimum of 4 (below). Acceptance under that rule is therefore not by itself evidence of $\eps$-optimality, and those runs did not keep their certificates.

\paragraph*{The checker the results use.} The $\eps$-checker (\texttt{check\_epsilon}) decides the $\eps$-claim itself. It converts every weight and dual value to an exact rational; refuses non-finite or negative values, sets that are not $\T$-odd, and corrections that do not explain the syndrome; computes $\theta$ and $\eps(J,z)$ exactly; and returns one of five outcomes: \emph{exactly optimal} ($\eps=0$), \emph{$\eps$-optimal} ($0<\eps\le\eps_{\max}$, with $\eps$ reported), \emph{not proven} ($\eps>\eps_{\max}$), \emph{invalid input}, or \emph{verifier failure}, a bound above the correction's weight, which is impossible for valid input and never an acceptance. A refusal is not evidence that the decoder erred; only a lighter valid correction is. The headline rates of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} are acceptances of this checker with $\eps_{\max}=10^{-6}$, from complete reruns that kept every receipt, and every receipt is re-decided by a second verifier written separately in scaled-integer arithmetic. The second verifier reads the receipt's list of sets itself, refusing a repeated set, rather than the first checker's parsed form, and the replay stops on the first receipt where the two verifiers, or either and the recorded verdict, disagree; a completed replay therefore means both returned the recorded verdict, slack and bound on every receipt. The checker's tests kill \MutEpsKilled\ of \MutEpsRun\ mutants generated mechanically by standard mutation operators; each of the remaining \MutEpsEquivalent\ computes the same function as the original, for a reason recorded with the score. Rates from the original runs are labelled as tolerance-rule acceptances wherever they appear.

\paragraph*{The audit of the original runs.} Those runs did not store their certificates, so their per-shot slack cannot be recovered. To measure what the rule actually certified, we re-ran the exact code that produced each headline artifact, extracted from its commit, on seeded samples of six configurations, kept every certificate it accepted, and analysed each in exact rational arithmetic. Table~\ref{tab:semantics} reports the result. None of the \AuditAccepted\ accepted certificates was inadmissible: all values were finite and non-negative, every set was $\T$-odd, and every correction explained its syndrome. The largest proven slack was \AuditEpsMax. On the three samples from the gap-distribution experiment, the re-run reproduced the artifact's certified counts exactly.

\paragraph*{Exact certification is a different and harder claim.} The released code also contains the exact checker, \texttt{matching\_cert.check}, the checker of the original runs (where it applied the tolerance rule), which now accepts only a proof of exact optimality: it repairs the floating-point rounding of a packing in exact arithmetic and then verifies feasibility and attainment from scratch. It accepts \AuditExactSame\ of the \AuditAccepted\ certificates above (\AuditExactSamePct\%), and the fraction falls steeply with graph size: most of the certificates on small graphs, but few on the 25-round simulation and only a handful on the largest hardware configuration (Table~\ref{tab:semantics}). Exact optimality over double-valued weights is therefore rarely provable at scale; we do not claim it, and the reruns count it separately. Reproducing the original tolerance-rule rates requires the code at the commits listed in Table~\ref{tab:semantics}. The refusals occur at the scale of the last few bits of a double. Part of that is the decoder's own: PyMatching matches on internally discretised weights, and among the \DetCleanN\ unmodified corrections of Sec.~\ref{sec:detection}, \DetCleanPymExcessPositive\ exceeded the exact optimum over the double-valued weights by a positive amount of at most $10^{-6}$, so refusals by an exact checker mix decoder rounding with producer slack. On the audited simulation samples, an independent exact-scored matching found PyMatching's correction heavier than an alternative by at most \AuditOracleMaxDelta, and equal on \AuditOracleEqual\ of \AuditOracleN\ shots; that oracle breaks near-ties in floating point, so it cannot settle every case. Because a decoder that computes in floating point is not attempting exact optimality over double-valued weights, and because those weights are estimates far coarser than $10^{-13}$, we regard $\eps$-optimality with a small, proven $\eps$ as the operationally meaningful claim, and report exact certification as a separate, stricter measurement rather than blending the two.

\paragraph*{A defect in the checkers, found and fixed.} Until 2026-10-18 the exact checker (\texttt{matching\_cert.check}), in every version including the one that applied the tolerance rule in the original runs, never tested that every set of a packing is $\T$-odd; a stricter variant (\texttt{matching\_cert\_strict}), which decides no number in this paper, tested oddness from its creation on 2026-08-17 against the fired detectors alone, without the boundary that $\T$ includes when their number is odd. Forged certificates that a linear-programming search over small graphs constructed against them exploit both. They are packings over sets that are not $\T$-join cut constraints, and they certify corrections heavier than the optimum: weight 6, 7 and 5 against optima of 4, 6 and 4. The exact checker accepted all three and the stricter variant the first two; both were fixed in commit \texttt{617da3af}. The three are now conformance vectors that the exact and the JavaScript checker must both refuse. The JavaScript checker always refused them, so the two implementations had disagreed on these inputs, and no vector contained one. The $\eps$-checker has tested $\T$-oddness since its first version, and a test holds it to refusing all three; the forgery battery of the runs did not include a class of this kind. Every headline rate of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is an acceptance of the $\eps$-checker and does not depend on the defect. The rates of the original runs, kept in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} as a development record, were accepted by the defective checker. Those runs kept no certificates, so the only evidence on this point is the audit above, which found all \AuditAccepted\ certificates it re-ran admissible. It re-ran every acceptance of the gap experiment of Sec.~\ref{sec:gaps}, so none of those was inadmissible. Of the other runs it sampled three, which bounds the inadmissible share of their acceptances below \AuditBoundSimPct\% for the simulation, \AuditBoundWFiveXPct\% for Willow at distance 5 over 13 rounds (X) and \AuditBoundWSevenXPct\% at distance 7 over 30 rounds (X), one-sided 95\% each; no other configuration or generation was sampled, and for those the audit gives no bound. Every count in this paper that an exact checker decided was recomputed with the fixed checkers and is unchanged: the exact-optimality columns of Table~\ref{tab:semantics}, the exact-rule gaps of Sec.~\ref{sec:gaps}, and the certified counts of Sec.~\ref{sec:measured-model}. Each of those artifacts now records the commit and the bytes of the checkers that decided it. The IBM run of Sec.~\ref{sec:other-hw} was re-certified under the $\eps$-checker.

\section{Producing the witness}
\label{sec:producer}

Producing a packing is the expensive half. \emph{Moat growth} grows every $\T$-odd component at equal rate and merges components when an edge becomes tight, as in the primal--dual approximation of Goemans and Williamson~\cite{goemans1995general}; unlike Edmonds' exact blossom algorithm~\cite{edmonds1965paths}, it does not revise its choices. It is feasible by construction and \GrowthSpeedupLo--\GrowthSpeedupHi\ times faster than solving a linear program, but at distance~7 it certifies \GrowthDSevenCertified\ shots against \LpDSevenCertified\ for the linear program, on the same shots (tolerance rule, development), because greedy merging picks the wrong pairs.

The main producer solves~\eqref{eq:packing} restricted to a candidate family of sets. Restricting the family cannot cost soundness, since a feasible point of the restricted problem is feasible for the full one; it costs only tightness, which appears as a reported gap. Families of balls around single detectors saturate on long memory experiments, because a ball cannot express a moat around a \emph{group} of detectors. The groups are read from the packing's own slack: a set can carry more value only if every edge on its boundary has slack, so the connected components of the subgraph of tight edges are the sets worth adding. The producer solves, adds those components, and solves again; the objective cannot decrease.

The producer is organised as a ladder whose rungs are tried cheapest first, escalating only on shots the previous rung left open. Generations of the ladder were each introduced against the shots the previous generation could not close: tight-component refinement; sets that cut the correction exactly once, as complementary slackness requires of an optimal dual; candidates translated from the duals of a blossom matching on the metric closure over $\T$; an escalation tier that raises every binding budget on refused shots; and a final exact tier that generates $\T$-odd cuts in the decoding graph by Padberg--Rao separation~\cite{padbergrao1982}, whose bound equals the minimum $\T$-join weight when the separation converges; the tier is capped at a round budget, and a shot it leaves open is reported as not proven (Sec.~\ref{sec:results} names two). The correction never changes between rungs, and every rung is judged by the same checker, so escalation can tighten a bound but cannot turn a wrong correction into a certified one. We call the rungs up to the blossom candidates the \emph{base ladder}, and the base ladder followed by the escalation and exact tiers the \emph{full ladder}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Rates proven under the $\eps$-checker are rounded down throughout, never up.

\subsection{Simulation at scale}

We sampled \EpsSimShots\ shots of a distance-5 rotated surface-code memory over 25 rounds at circuit-level depolarising noise $p=0.005$, generated with Stim~\cite{gidney2021stim} and decoded with PyMatching~\cite{higgott2025sparse} on a graph of \SimDetectors\ detectors plus the boundary and \SimEdges\ edges, following the seed plan of the original run's main pass chunk for chunk. Each correction went through the base ladder of Sec.~\ref{sec:producer} and was judged by the checker of Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}. Every chunk completed, a failed chunk being retried until it did (\EpsSimChunkRetries\ retries), and every receipt was kept. Of \EpsSimNontrivial\ nontrivial corrections, \EpsSimAccepted\ (\EpsSimAcceptedPct\%) were proven within $\eps_{\max}=10^{-6}$ of optimal, \EpsSimExact\ of them exactly, with a largest proven slack of \EpsSimLargestEps; the remaining \EpsSimNotProven\ (\EpsSimNotProvenPct\%) were not proven. Whether the producer's development touched this seed plan is not recorded, so we certified \FreshSimN\ further shots of the same configuration, drawn after the first review round of this manuscript from seeds far from every recorded seed base of the simulation runs: \FreshSimAccepted\ (\FreshSimPct\%; 95\% interval \FreshSimCILo--\FreshSimCIHi\%) were proven, \FreshSimNotProven\ were not, none of \FreshSimForgeries\ forgeries was accepted, and every receipt replays under both verifiers (\texttt{fresh\_seed\_sim.py}; post hoc). They were certified by the producer that certified the held-out sample below: run again, it reproduces all \IdentFreshChunks\ of their receipt files byte for byte. ``Exactly'' here means $\eps(J,z)=0$ for the packing as submitted, under the $\eps$-checker; the exact column of Table~\ref{tab:semantics} is a different test, the exact checker applied to the original runs' certificates, and the two fractions are not comparable.

\paragraph*{The original run.} The same experiment under the tolerance rule accepted \SimCertified\ of \SimShots\ corrections (\SimCertifiedPct\%). It kept no certificates, and its count was completed from supplements with disjoint seeds after \SimFailedChunks\ worker chunks (\SimShotsLostToChunks\ shots) died to memory exhaustion on a shared workstation. Some of those deaths were allocation failures during certification, whose memory use depends on the syndrome, so that rate cannot exclude selection by difficulty; the rerun has no such gap.

\paragraph*{The forgery battery, and why its zero means something.} For every shot with a packing, certified or not, we built forged certificates from the strongest packing found, in up to \EpsSimForgeryClasses\ classes as applicable: corrections with a dropped, duplicated, or mismatched edge; strictly heavier corrections; corrections with a spurious cycle; a heavier correction with the packing scaled to meet its weight; the same with a negative value hiding the inflation; and the genuine packing with one value replaced by NaN or by $+\infty$. Of \EpsSimForgeries\ forgeries (eight classes on each of the $10^6$ shots, plus a strictly heavier valid correction on the single shot to which that class applied), none was accepted. Twice during development an ``attack'' was accepted and the checker was right: once the forged correction was an equal-weight alternative optimum, and once a construction meant to hide a negative value degenerated into a valid certificate for an optimal correction. Both were attacks whose claim happened to be true. Every forgery other than the non-finite ones is therefore re-verified to be false, independently of the checker, before its verdict is counted, and a run in which any forgery fails that test is void. The replay of the kept receipts adds attacks at the acceptance boundary itself: one correction edge's weight is raised to the smallest double that puts the proven slack past $\eps_{\max}$, which both verifiers must refuse, or by half the remaining room, which both must accept because the claim is then true; and receipts are replayed against another configuration's graph, where none may be accepted. These counts show that the attacks tried were refused. That no forgery can succeed is what the lemma says, not what the counts show.

\subsection{Is the decoder, or the producer, responsible for refusals?}
\label{sec:oracle}

A refusal can mean the decoder's correction was not optimal or that the producer failed to find a tight packing. To separate the two we computed, for every nontrivial shot of three simulated configurations, a minimum-weight perfect matching on the metric closure over $\T$ with per-detector boundary copies, using networkx~\cite{hagberg2008networkx}: a different formulation and algorithm, sharing no code with the producer. On all \OracleShots\ shots (\OracleShotsThree\ at distance 3 over \OracleRoundsThree\ rounds, \OracleShotsFive\ at distance 5 and \OracleShotsSeven\ at distance 7, both over 25 rounds) the oracle's weight equalled the decoder's, so every refusal on those shots was producer slack. The oracle breaks near-ties in floating point, so this agreement holds to floating-point resolution, far inside $\eps_{\max}$; Sec.~\ref{sec:detection} repeats the separation with an exact-arithmetic oracle.

\subsection{Other hardware}
\label{sec:other-hw}

To check that nothing is specific to one vendor's data format or graph construction, we certified a repetition-code memory run on IBM's \IbmBackend\ processor, preregistered before submission (SHA-256 prefix \texttt{\IbmPreregPrefix}): the full ladder's certificates for all \IbmCertified\ of \IbmNontrivial\ nontrivial shots were accepted, at \IbmMsPerShot\ ms per shot, under the tolerance rule of the original runs, whose checker did not test $\T$-oddness (Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}), and they were not kept. We re-certified the run from the job's raw registers, read again from the provider and now released, which reproduce the same \IbmNontrivial\ nontrivial shots: with the same producer, all \IbmEpsAccepted\ were proven within $\eps_{\max}$ of optimal by the $\eps$-checker, \IbmEpsExact\ of them exactly, both verifiers returned the same verdict, slack and bound on every receipt, and none of \IbmEpsForgeries\ forgeries was accepted (\texttt{ibm\_epsilon\_rerun.py}). Here the error model is ours, a declared uniform model, not the experimenters', so these shots are reported separately from every total above.

\subsection{Certifying against a model measured from the run itself}
\label{sec:measured-model}

Every run above certifies against weights supplied from outside the shots being decoded: the experimenters' model on Willow, a declared uniform model on IBM. A certificate proves minimum weight with respect to the weights it is given and is indifferent to where they came from, so the sharper question is whether the accept path can honour a model estimated from the device's own syndromes, which is the form a deployed system would actually want. That model is not ours: the two-point estimator that recovers pairwise error probabilities from detector correlations alone, with no calibration input and no access to logical outcomes, is due to Spitz \textit{et al.}~\cite{spitz2018adaptive} and is the analysis Google applied to repetition codes~\cite{chen2021exponential}.

We estimated a decoding graph from the first half of each run's detectors and certified the second half against it, on \CertMeasRecords\ preregistered hardware records across \CertMeasDevices\ IBM processors, at distances 3 and 5 and at 13 and 40 rounds, \CertMeasHeldOut\ held-out shots in total. Of \CertMeasNontrivial\ nontrivial held-out shots, \CertMeasCertified\ received a certificate of exact minimum weight under the estimated graph, which carries between \CertMeasEdgesLo\ and \CertMeasEdgesHi\ edges depending on the record. These certificates are decided by the exact checker of Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}, not the $\eps$-checker: on graphs this small its rational repair usually closes, which it rarely does at the scale of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. The certificates of this section were not kept or replayed, and the forgery battery was not run on them, so they carry less evidence than the receipts above. The \CertMeasRefused\ refusals all fall on the two 40-round records, where the checker declines a float-weighted instance rather than accepting it; those shots are reported as unknown to this tier, as everywhere else in this paper. On every record the accept path and the campaign's own scorer return identical logical-error counts, which is checked before any number here is generated, because two decoders that should agree and are never compared is how a convention drifts.

We do not report how much better the estimated graph decodes than the models it replaces: on these short repetition-code records the baseline models' logical error rates are poor enough that the comparison would say more about the baselines than about the estimator, and we have not established why. Estimated models outperforming device-informed ones is documented elsewhere, including on IBM hardware~\cite{takou2026logical}, and a calibration-conditioned neural decoder beats calibration-weighted matching by up to $11.1\times$ at distance 11 on these same processors~\cite{stein2026calibration}; no priority is claimed for the estimator or for any decoding margin it brings.

What the searches logged with the released artifacts did not find is the combination: a per-shot, third-party-checkable optimality witness attached to a model \emph{estimated from the shots being decoded}. This section produced such witnesses but did not keep them. Not finding it is not proof of absence. The caveat of Sec.~\ref{sec:related} applies with full force here, because the temptation runs the other way: certifying against a measured model does not make the measured model true. It makes the decoder's answer checkable under that model, and nothing more.

\section{What the certificate detects}
\label{sec:detection}

\subsection{Seeded decoder faults}

What a certificate can detect is fixed in advance by the lemma: a packing proves at most the optimum, so no packing certifies a correction heavier than the optimum by more than $\eps_{\max}$, and a sound checker refuses every such correction. The experiments below therefore do not measure detection power in the usual sense. They test that the implemented checker behaves as the lemma says on realistic faults, and they measure what the lemma cannot fix: the false-alarm rate, the refusals of corrections that are in fact within $\eps_{\max}$ of optimal. The lighter correction that proves a refused one suboptimal comes here from an exact oracle, not from the certificate; a certificate alone says ``not proven'', and only a lighter valid correction says ``suboptimal''.

We mutated the decoder's output at distances 3, 5 and 7 with defects of several kinds, which models wrong answers but not crashes, hangs, or memory errors, and judged every correction against the exact minimum-weight correction, computed by an exact-arithmetic oracle whose answer is re-verified before use. Each correction receives one outcome under the $\eps$-checker of Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics} with $\eps_{\max}=10^{-6}$: \emph{invalid}, when it does not explain the syndrome; \emph{certified}; \emph{proven suboptimal}, when it is refused and a lighter valid correction is exhibited; or \emph{not proven}, when it is refused without such a witness, which says nothing about the decoder. Of \DetDefectN\ defective corrections, \DetDefectInvalid\ were invalid, which syndrome checking also detects, and the other \DetDefectProven\ were proven suboptimal; none was certified and none was left not proven. Those \DetStealthN\ are the informative subset: faults constructed to leave the syndrome exactly satisfied while making the correction strictly heavier, invisible to syndrome checking by construction. The measurement is the false-alarm rate. On the \DetCleanN\ unmodified corrections of the same shots, the base ladder refused \DetCleanFalseAlarms\ (95\% interval \CIFaBaseLo--\CIFaBaseHi\%) and the full ladder \DetCleanFalseAlarmsFull\ (at most \CIFaFullHi\%), and every such refusal was producer slack on a correction within $\eps_{\max}$ of optimal. As a negative control, all \DetControlN\ equal-weight alternative optima, generated at distances 3 and 5 (none arose at distance 7), were certified (a false-alarm rate of at most \CICtrlHi\% on them). The full corpus of \FaultCorpus\ seeded cases is released so other detectors can be scored on the same faults.

This experiment is open to the objection that the mutations were chosen knowing what the certificate checks. The next one is not, although it needs a reference of its own.

\subsection{A stale calibration}

Here the decoder runs unmodified and its output is never touched. The fault is upstream: the decoder decodes on a graph built from a drifted error model, as it would if its calibration snapshot were stale, and its corrections are checked against a reference model supplied independently, here the model that generated the data. A drift factor $f$ multiplies each error mechanism's probability by an independent factor $e^{U}$, with $U$ uniform on $[-\ln f,\ln f]$, clipped to $[10^{-12},0.49]$, and the decoder's matching graph is rebuilt from the drifted model. Stale snapshots arise when calibration jobs fail and the previous snapshot is reused, or when a deployment points at the wrong run. Every correction is syndrome-consistent by construction, because the decoder solves a correct problem with wrong weights. What a certificate detects here is a mismatch between the objective the decoder optimised and the reference; it cannot say which of the two describes the device, so in deployment its value rests on the reference being the better model.

\section{Per-shot optimality gaps}
\label{sec:gaps}

Decoders are usually compared by logical error rate, an average that cannot distinguish a decoder that is consistently slightly suboptimal from one that is usually exact with a heavy tail. A certified optimum on the same shot makes the individual gap measurable. Restricting to shots whose minimum-weight correction was certified, the bound equals the optimum and each decoder's gap is its excess weight. We sampled \GapShotsPerDistance\ shots per distance with rounds equal to distance at $p=0.005$ and decoded each with BP-OSD~\cite{panteleev2021degenerate,roffe2020decoding} and belief-matching~\cite{higgott2023improved}; Table~\ref{tab:gaps} reports BP-OSD. BP-OSD decodes the check matrix whose columns are the graphlike components of the decomposed error model, and the components it selects are scored with the matching graph's weights, the currency of the bound, so a gap never compares two different weightings; no gap was negative.

\section{Related work and scope}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph*{Certifying and checking.} Certifying algorithms~\cite{mcconnell2011certifying} and program checkers~\cite{blumkannan1995} are classical, as is the linear-programming structure of matching and $\T$-joins~\cite{edmonds1965polyhedron,edmondsjohnson1973}. In exact integer programming, the gap between a floating-point solver's claim and a proof is well studied, with safe dual bounds and verifiable certificates~\cite{steffy2013valid,cheung2017verifying,eifler2023computational}; Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics} addresses the same issue for decoding receipts.

\paragraph*{Decoding.} Wu \textit{et al.}~\cite{wu2025minimum} formulate most-likely-error decoding as a minimum-weight parity factor problem and decode with a primal--dual algorithm that emits a proximity bound with every solution; a dual that meets the primal's weight certifies optimality, so a decoder that produces its own optimality certificate per shot already exists, and the observation that decoding has a checkable dual is not new. What differs here is the setting: a certificate produced after the fact for a third-party decoder's correction, decided by a separately written checker in exact arithmetic, with the cost of producing it measured (Sec.~\ref{sec:cost}). Linear-programming decoding of classical and quantum codes has a long history~\cite{feldman2005lp,li2018lp}.

\paragraph*{Concurrent work.} Three recent preprints sit close to this one. Krishnamoorthy \textit{et al.}~\cite{krishnamoorthy2026certified} decide degenerate-coset maximum likelihood on surface and bivariate-bicycle codes and, by mini-bucket elimination, deterministically certify most of their tested decisions with machine-checkable optimality proofs; their objective is coset maximum likelihood, not the minimum-weight correction certified here, and their exact certificates cost minutes per decode. Shen and Zhong~\cite{shen2026capability} test decoder libraries for conformance and release an oracle-free suboptimality certificate, two feasible corrections of different weight, re-derived by a standalone verifier from first principles; that is the witness this paper calls ``proven suboptimal'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:detection}), and the lower bound that certifies optimality is what the receipts here add to it. Ehatamm \textit{et al.}~\cite{ehatamm2026end} formalise quantum error correction end to end in a proof assistant, verifying the framework rather than individual instances. Our literature searches, whose queries are logged with the released artifacts, did not find per-shot $\eps$-optimality receipts for a matching decoder on hardware data at this scale, kept and replayed, nor the forgery battery; not finding them is not proof of absence.

\paragraph*{What is not claimed.} Certification is not a real-time decoder (Sec.~\ref{sec:cost}). It is not complete: \EpsSimNotProvenPct\% of simulated corrections and \EpsHwNotProven\ hardware corrections were not proven, and those receive receipts stating their proven slack. The rates are properties of our producer, a decoder, and an error model; soundness is a property of the checker alone. The headline rates are rates of proven $\eps$-optimality in the sense of Sec.~\ref{sec:semantics}; exact optimality is counted separately and not claimed at scale. A certificate proves optimality for the weights it was given, and says nothing about whether those weights describe the device. Nor can it separate tied optima: a decoder change that selects a different equal-weight correction, possibly in a different logical class, passes unchanged, as the negative control of Sec.~\ref{sec:detection} shows by design.

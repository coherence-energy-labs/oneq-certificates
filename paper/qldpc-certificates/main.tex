\title{Exact, independently checkable optimality receipts for BP-OSD on the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code}

\OwnerAuthor
\OwnerAffiliation

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
General quantum decoding is NP-hard, so no polynomial-time decoder is expected to return an optimum on every instance; that does not rule out checkable optimality witnesses on the instances that arise in practice. We attach to each syndrome decoded by BP-OSD a dual solution of the Feldman--Wainwright--Karger linear-programming relaxation adapted to syndromes, verified in exact rational arithmetic from the check matrix, the weights and the syndrome alone, with no solver and no trust in the producer. When the exact lower bound excludes every smaller objective value on the weight lattice, the correction is minimum-weight. When the relaxation is not tight, a tree of such duals over a partition of the feasible set closes the proof. On the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code, in a preregistered campaign with seeds derived deterministically from a frozen commit, all \HoNontrivial\ nontrivial simulated syndromes across \HoRungs\ datasets, from code-capacity noise to circuit-level noise under the code's published syndrome-extraction schedule (three rounds, one declared observable, idle locations unfaulted), received an exact receipt for the declared objective, \HoFlat\ as flat certificates and \HoTrees\ as branch-dual trees, \HoTreesOneNode\ of them a single dual. All \ReplayReceipts\ stored receipts replay completely against a pinned manifest, each accepted by two separately written checkers. Because a receipt fixes the optimum, it measures a decoder without access to the true error: the campaign's BP-OSD missed the certified optimum on \HoPostStressMissRecorded\ of \HoPostStressN\ syndromes at the stress noise level. A post-hoc check prompted by review traced this to the default min-sum scaling factor, 1, of the ldpc library's decoder class; re-decoded with scaling \ScaledAlpha\ and nothing else changed, BP-OSD missed the optimum on \HoPostStressMissScaled. Receipts certify objective optimality only; tied optima can carry different logical frames. All results are simulations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Decoding a stabilizer code is NP-hard in general~\cite{berlekamp1978,hsieh2011np}, and belief propagation with ordered-statistics post-processing (BP-OSD)~\cite{panteleev2021degenerate,roffe2020decoding} is a standard heuristic for quantum low-density parity-check codes, including the bivariate-bicycle codes proposed as near-term memories~\cite{bravyi2024high}. A BP-OSD implementation returns a correction without a witness of its quality, and the only property routinely checked, that the correction explains the syndrome, is satisfied equally by an optimal and a suboptimal answer.

Hardness does not preclude witnesses on particular instances. A companion manuscript, \textit{Certifying quantum error-correction decoders}, released with this one (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}), attached linear-programming duals to matching decoders and audited them at scale; those receipts proved optimality to within a small additive slack under floating-point weights. This paper asks whether the same discipline survives the move to a decoding objective that is not treated here as a single graphlike $T$-join problem (matching-based decoders for bivariate-bicycle codes do exist~\cite{sahay2026matching}; they solve a different problem than the one certified here), and demands a stronger receipt: an exact rational proof under a published integer objective. We make three contributions. First, a receipt system for BP-OSD built from the Feldman--Wainwright--Karger (FWK) relaxation~\cite{feldman2005lp}, checked in exact arithmetic by a checker that imports no producer code, with a certified repair when BP-OSD is refused and branch-dual trees when the relaxation is not tight. Second, a preregistered, frozen measurement of receipt coverage on the [[144,12,12]] code, including circuit-level noise under the code's published syndrome-extraction schedule. Third, a use of the receipts as an instrument that measures how often a decoder's answer is optimal without access to the true error; with it, a strong-baseline test and a post-hoc re-analysis prompted by review trace most of the BP-OSD answers the receipts proved suboptimal to the library's default, unscaled min-sum, not to BP-OSD as such. Every number is a simulation; Sec.~\ref{sec:scope} states what that does and does not limit.

\section{Problem and relaxation}
\label{sec:problem}

Given a sparse check matrix $H$ over $\GF$ with rows (checks) $j$ of support $N(j)$, non-negative rational weights $w$, and a syndrome $s$, the decoding problem is to find $e\in\{0,1\}^n$ minimising $w^\top e$ subject to $He=s \pmod 2$. With $w_i=1$ this is minimum Hamming weight. For heterogeneous and circuit-level noise we publish an integer weight vector, generated as $c_i=\lfloor 1000\log((1-p_i)/p_i)+\tfrac12\rfloor$, and that vector, not the logarithms it approximates, is the normative objective. Changing the scale factor through $10^3$, $10^4$ and $10^5$ on identical syndromes certified \ScaleCertified\ shots with \ScaleSupportChanges\ changes in any selected correction. Minimum weight under independent mechanism weights is not maximum likelihood over degenerate cosets, and every claim below is conformance to this stated specification.

\paragraph*{Relaxation.} Each check $j$ is relaxed to its local codeword polytope~\cite{feldman2005lp}, adapted to the syndrome bit: for every $F\subseteq N(j)$ with $|F|\not\equiv s_j \pmod 2$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in F}(1-x_i)+\sum_{i\in N(j)\setminus F}x_i\;\ge\;1 .
\label{eq:facet}
\end{equation}
Minimising $w^\top x$ over these constraints and $[0,1]^n$ lower-bounds the integer optimum, so any feasible dual solution certifies a lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[safe dual]
Write the validated facets as $a_r^\top x\ge b_r$. For any $y\ge0$, let $z_i=\max\bigl(0,(A^\top y)_i-w_i\bigr)$. Then $L=\sum_r b_r y_r-\sum_i z_i$ is a lower bound on $w^\top e$ for every feasible $e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $x\in[0,1]^n$ satisfying the facets, $w^\top x\ge (A^\top y)^\top x-z^\top x\ge y^\top Ax-\sum_i z_i\ge b^\top y-\sum_i z_i$.
\end{proof}
The lemma holds for every $y\ge 0$, so the checker needs no feasibility tolerance: it recomputes $z$ and $L$ exactly from whatever multipliers the certificate lists. If all weights lie in $(1/D)\mathbb{Z}$, every feasible objective lies on that lattice, and a lower bound $L>U-1/D$ proves a feasible candidate of objective $U$ optimal; $L>U$ is an instrument failure and is reported as one.

\section{The receipt ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

Every shot receives a receipt naming its tier.
\begin{description}
\item[Tier 0.] For $s=0$ the empty correction is optimal at cost zero, because the checker refuses negative weights. This certifies the objective, not the absence of an undetected logical fault. Trivial shots are excluded from the nontrivial rates below.
\item[Tier 1, certified optimal.] The checker verifies $He=s$, reconstructs every facet and cut, computes $L$ exactly from the dual of the shot's flat relaxation, and verifies $L>U-1/D$ for BP-OSD's answer.
\item[Tier 2, certified repair.] When BP-OSD's answer is refused and the relaxation's primal is integral, that primal is a minimum-weight correction certified by the same dual under the same rule~\cite{feldman2005lp}; the checker re-verifies $He=s$ from scratch. In the instance that exposed this tier, BP-OSD returned weight \DiscBpWeight\ against a tight bound of \DiscBound; the difference was a weight-\DiscFaultWeight\ logical operator, and the certified repair lay in the same coset as the sampled fault.
\item[Tier BD, branch duals.] When neither applies, a mixed-integer solver proposes a candidate and the receipt is a tree: branching on a coordinate partitions the binary feasible set into $x_k=0$ and $x_k=1$, each again a syndrome instance, and every leaf carries either a safe dual proving the bound on the reduced instance or a $\GF$ row combination showing that no completion exists. The checker walks the tree in exact arithmetic without a solver; nothing the solver reports is trusted. A tree may be a single node: a dual that proves the candidate optimal by the lattice rule although the relaxation's primal is fractional, so that the integral-primal repair of tier 2 did not apply.
\end{description}
Redundant parity checks, sums of rows over $\GF$, are generated adaptively in the manner of Zhang and Siegel~\cite{zhangsiegel2012adaptive}. Each cut is named by the rows it sums, and the checker re-derives its support and parity from $(H,s)$.

Two soundness statements are kept separate. The mathematical one: under the declared parsing and rational-domain assumptions, checker acceptance implies objective optimality, by the lemma and the partition argument. The implementation one: the checker is separately reimplemented, mutation-tested, and exercised on the released corpus. The second is strong evidence, not formal verification.

\section{The checker}
\label{sec:checker}

The exact checker admits only rationals, rejecting floating-point values by type, and imports nothing beyond the Python standard library's \texttt{fractions}, \texttt{math}, \texttt{operator} and \texttt{dataclasses}; producers may use anything. The same module also carries a floating-point \emph{screen} that the producer consults before spending an exactification on a candidate; no receipt is accepted on the screen's verdict, and every count in this paper is the exact checker's. Verification costs $O(\mathrm{nnz}(H_R)+\mathrm{nnz}(C)+n)$ arithmetic operations for the reconstructed redundant rows $H_R$ and listed facet--multiplier pairs $C$, with bit complexity governed by the size of the rationals. Held-out certificates listed at most \HoMaxFacets\ facets, with numerators of at most \HoMaxNumBits\ bits and denominators of at most \HoMaxDenBits\ bits. Median exact checking time per shot ranged from \TimeExactCheckMedLo\ to \TimeExactCheckMedHi\ ms across datasets, against median producer times of \TimeLpMedLo\ to \TimeLpMedHi\ ms.

Independence is limited, and we state its extent precisely. A reference checker for flat certificates was written from the mathematical specification without importing or inspecting the producer or the production checker, and is pinned by hash; a second tree checker was written the same way from the tree schema. Both were written within the same project by the same author, in the same language as the production checker, and ran on the same workstation: they are separate implementations, not independent third-party reproductions, and agreement between them establishes agreement on the tested inputs, not the absence of a shared misreading of the specification. Third checkers written in JavaScript from the specification, by the same author with AI assistance and without consulting either Python file, with exact rational arithmetic and no dependencies, accept all \JsFlatAccepted\ flat receipts and refuse all \JsDefects\ planted defects in \JsDefectClasses\ classes, each for its own reason, and accept all \JsTreeAccepted\ stored trees and refuse all \JsTreeDefects\ planted tree defects in \JsTreeDefectClasses\ classes; no stored tree closes a leaf by infeasibility, sits on the lattice boundary or overloads a multiplier, so those rules are exercised both ways on a hand-built instance whose one branch is provably infeasible, judged by the production checker as well. Writing the third checkers exposed no ambiguity in the specification; it exposed those three rules of the harness that no stored receipt reached, which two deliberately broken copies of the tree checker passed until the hand-built instance was added. An external audit then found that both JavaScript programs, having written their verdicts, exited before a piped standard output had drained, delivering 65{,}536 bytes of a larger batch with exit code 0; the harnesses caught it only because they reconcile receipt identifiers, and the programs now let the process end on its own. The checker's source is exercised by a hand-chosen set of targeted mutants, each of which a named test must detect: a sign inversion, a deleted feasibility pass, a syndrome taken on faith on each path that recomputes it, a forgotten parity on redundant checks, a loosened facet-parity rule, a removed slack derivation, a duplicate facet, and a lattice rule loosened by one level. That set shows the tests catch the defects we anticipated. To measure what they catch beyond it, we applied standard mutation operators at every eligible site of the checker module: of \MutQRun\ mutants the tests kill \MutQKilled, and each of the remaining \MutQEquivalent\ computes the same function as the original, for a reason recorded with the score. Reaching that took new tests, and one surviving mutant exposed a defect in the floating-point screen, a path no receipt passes through: it admitted negative weights within its tolerance and could then prove a correction optimal while a lighter one existed. The screen now refuses every negative weight; the exact and branch-dual checkers that decide every receipt always did. Certificate-level forgeries (a certificate replayed from another syndrome or matrix, a heavier feasible candidate, a floating-point multiplier, a dual inflated until its overload no longer proves the bound, an incomplete partition) are refused, and the second tree checker refuses all \TreeMutants\ of its forged-tree classes.

\section{Adaptive facet separation at circuit-level degrees}
\label{sec:separation}

Adaptive linear-programming decoding, which starts with no facets and repeatedly adds the most violated facet per check, is due to Taghavi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2008adaptive}, including the $O(\deg)$ separation rule and its exactness. We restate the rule for syndromes. Writing $\mathrm{LHS}(F)$ for the left side of~\eqref{eq:facet} and $F_0=\{i:x_i>\tfrac12\}$,
\[
\mathrm{LHS}(F)=\mathrm{LHS}(F_0)+\sum_{i\in F\triangle F_0}\bigl|2x_i-1\bigr| ,
\]
so the minimiser over the wrong-parity family is $F_0$ itself when $|F_0|$ already has the wrong parity, and otherwise $F_0$ with the coordinate nearest $\tfrac12$ toggled. The shipped rule matches brute force over all wrong-parity subsets on \SepInstances\ random instances with \SepSuboptimal\ suboptimal separations (largest discrepancy \SepWorstGap), and agrees with a separately written reference implementation on \SepRefInstances\ further instances, including exact ties at $\tfrac12$. At convergence no facet of the full family is violated, so the lazy relaxation equals full enumeration; on \EnsLazyCodes\ random codes where enumeration was feasible the two agreed to within \EnsLazyWorst.

This matters at circuit level, where detector degrees reach \SurfaceDegreeMax\ and a full facet family has $2^{46}$ rows. Degree-capped enumeration remains sound, since a subset of facets only enlarges the feasible region, and matched full tightness on \EnsCappedTight\ random codes; adaptive separation needs no cap. Deployed first, with enumeration only on refusal, it gave identical coverage on a circuit-level surface-code benchmark at \TieredMs\ against \EnumMs\ ms per shot, a factor of \TieredSpeedup.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Two sampling paths, both on a single workstation: the code-capacity, heterogeneous, phenomenological and X-sector datasets, and the strong-baseline experiment of Sec.~\ref{sec:strong}, draw independent faults with NumPy's generator under the stated rates; the circuit-level dataset samples its detector error model with Stim~\cite{gidney2021stim}. BP-OSD (\texttt{ldpc}~\LdpcVersion) used min-sum belief propagation with a parallel schedule, 30 iterations (40 at circuit level), and OSD-CS of order 5~\cite{roffe2020decoding}, with the default min-sum scaling factor of the library's \texttt{BpOsdDecoder} class, \LdpcClassAlpha, that is, no scaling. Sec.~\ref{sec:strong} shows that this default is the main reason BP-OSD's answers were often not optimal: at every declared error rate we tried, this configuration finds the certified optimum on \ScanFiveALo--\ScanFiveAHi\ of \StrongShots\ strong-baseline syndromes at $p=0.05$ and \ScanSevenALo--\ScanSevenAHi\ at $p=0.07$, and with the scaling factor set to \ScaledAlpha\ on \ScanFiveAs\ and \ScanSevenAs.

\subsection{Preregistered held-out campaign}
\label{sec:heldout}

The protocol, datasets, endpoints and seed rule were committed and tagged before any held-out shot was sampled. Each dataset's seed is the first 16 hexadecimal digits of $\mathrm{SHA256}(\texttt{"oneq-heldout-2026||"}+\text{dataset}+\texttt{"||"}+\text{freeze commit})$, masked to 63 bits and reduced modulo $2^{31}-1$ for the circuit dataset; all seeds re-derive from the tagged commit by this rule. The rule makes the samples replayable; it is not an external randomness beacon, and local evidence cannot exclude a search over alternative freeze states. Code and experiment directories are byte-identical between the freeze and results commits, and no shots were excluded or re-run. For the seven non-circuit datasets, sampling continued until the predeclared number of nontrivial syndromes was reached; the circuit dataset used a fixed 150 sampled shots, of which one was trivial: the predeclaration lists ``150'' for this rung beside nontrivial targets for the others, the runner took it as raw shots, and the 149 nontrivial are what is reported (a recorded deviation from the nontrivial rule, not a re-run). All \HoNontrivial\ nontrivial syndromes received exact receipts: \HoFlat\ flat and \HoTrees\ trees, \HoTreesOneNode\ of which are a single dual, so \HoNoBranch\ (\HoNoBranchPct\%) needed no branching. The flat receipts divide into \HoTierOne\ that certify BP-OSD's own answer and \HoTierTwo\ that certify a replacement. A tree proves the optimum's value, so a BP-OSD answer of that value is proven optimal too: with the \HoTreeBpOptimal\ tree shots on which BP-OSD's answer attains it, \HoBpProvenOptimal\ BP-OSD answers (\HoBpProvenOptimalPct\%) are proven optimal, while \HoFlatPct\% of syndromes needed no tree. A tier-2 receipt is stronger than ``not proven optimal'': its bound satisfies $L>U-1/D$ for the replacement and BP-OSD's answer is heavier, so each of the \HoTierTwo\ is a BP-OSD answer proven strictly heavier than the optimum. That is a property of the decoder as the campaign configured it. A post-hoc re-analysis prompted by review (\texttt{qldpc\_heldout\_scaled\_posthoc.py}; not preregistered) regenerates every sampled fault by the seed rule and first reproduces the campaign: its own decoder returns every stored BP-OSD answer, and every recorded logical outcome is recomputed, on all four uniform-weight code-capacity datasets. Then, with the min-sum scaling factor set to \ScaledAlpha\ and every other setting unchanged, the number of BP-OSD answers at the certified optimum rises from \HoPostStressRecorded\ to \HoPostStressScaled\ of \HoPostStressN\ on the stress dataset, from \HoPostUniTwoRecorded\ to \HoPostUniTwoScaled\ of \HoPostUniTwoN\ at $p=0.02$, and from \HoPostXTwoRecorded\ to \HoPostXTwoScaled\ of \HoPostXTwoN\ on the X-sector dataset; the receipts decide this without the true error. The campaign's misses are not an artifact of the floating-point form of its declared rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:strong}): told any of 16 error rates, the same decoder misses the optimum on \ScanHoAMissLo--\ScanHoAMissHi\ of the stress syndromes. The heterogeneous, phenomenological and circuit datasets use other weights or matrices and were not re-decoded, so this attribution does not extend to the \HoNotRedecodedTierTwo\ BP-OSD answers proven heavier there. The per-dataset bounds in Table~\ref{tab:heldout} concern certification coverage, not logical correctness, and are not a simultaneous guarantee. No receipt in these totals rests on a solver's reported status. Across held-out and development datasets, the \TreeCount\ stored trees had a median of \TreeNodesMedian\ nodes, a 95th percentile of \TreeNodesPNinetyFive, at most \TreeNodesMax\ nodes and depth \TreeDepthMax, and at most \TreeBytesMax\ serialised bytes. Tree size is exponential in the worst case, and this is not a polynomial-time exact decoder.

The complete stored corpus, \ReplayReceipts\ receipts (the \HoFlat\ held-out flat receipts, the \HoTrees\ held-out trees and \ReplayDevTrees\ development trees; the development flat certificates were not stored, and four of the development trees were stored after the fact and are included in the tree statistics above), replays against a manifest whose hash is pinned in the replay tool: every receipt is checked by two separately written checkers (Sec.~\ref{sec:checker}) against persisted, hash-bound check matrices and integer weight vectors, and the replay fails if any receipt or bound file is missing, duplicated, unlisted or altered. The persisted instances match the instance hashes recorded by the frozen run. After the campaign the checkers were changed to refuse any syndrome entry other than the integers 0 and 1, which they had previously reduced modulo 2; every stored syndrome is binary, and the complete corpus replays under the changed checkers.

\subsection{Circuit level under the published schedule}

The circuit dataset implements the code's published depth-8 syndrome-extraction cycle~\cite{bravyi2024high} in Stim, memory-Z over three rounds at uniform circuit-level noise $p=0.002$, with 288 detectors and 2{,}304 mechanisms under published integer weights; the noise family is ours and does not fault idle locations as the original error model does. The transcription was validated at the fault level, not only noiselessly: an operation generator written independently from a hashed layer manifest reproduces the circuit exactly (\XvalOps\ operations, \XvalRecords\ measurement records), and every single $X$ and $Z$ fault at every location, \XvalFaults\ in total, was propagated by a separately written symplectic engine and compared with Stim's detector and observable signature, with \XvalMismatches\ mismatches. The held-out run certified all \HoRefschedNontrivial\ nontrivial shots: \HoRefschedTierOne\ BP-OSD answers directly, \HoRefschedTierTwo\ certified repairs and \HoRefschedTrees\ trees (flat-only \HoRefschedFlatPct\%). The circuit declares one logical observable, and it agreed with the sampled truth on \HoRefschedObsAgree\ of \HoRefschedNontrivial\ shots; this is a result for that observable, not an all-12-logical-qubit block error, and zero observed disagreements in \HoRefschedNontrivial\ shots bounds the underlying disagreement rate only below \HoRefschedObsUpperPct\% (one-sided 95\%). BP-OSD's own answers, scored the same way, also agreed on \HoRefschedBposdObsAgree\ of \HoRefschedNontrivial\ shots: the certificate replaced \HoRefschedReplaced\ of them with a strictly lighter correction, and all \HoRefschedReplacedSameObs\ replacements lie in the same logical class as the answer they replaced, so at this noise level the certificate changed the weight of the answer and never its outcome. That number is derived from the stored records rather than re-sampled: each record keeps BP-OSD's candidate beside the certified answer, both satisfy the same syndrome, and the observable row of the pinned model decides whether their difference crosses the logical (\texttt{tools/bposd\_circuit\_outcome.py}).

Two further circuit datasets, a surface-code control and a custom sequential schedule, were checked only in floating point. Floating-point acceptance is not a receipt in the sense of this paper, and they are not counted here.

\paragraph*{Where the method stops.} The same tree machinery bounds the weight $d_{\mathrm{DEM}}$ of the lightest undetectable fault that flips the declared observable of the circuit dataset. A weight-\DistUpper\ fault found by Stim's search fires no detector and flips the observable, so $d_{\mathrm{DEM}}\le\DistUpper$ for this observable and noise family. Over all twelve logical observables the circuit distance of this cycle is 10, a value since found, each under its own noise model, by an exhaustive search for twelve noisy rounds~\cite{crocker2026gross} and by exact enumeration for up to three rounds~\cite{pan2026interleaving}, neither recording a certificate. A second companion manuscript released with this one, \emph{Machine-checkable lower-bound certificates for the circuit-level distances of bivariate-bicycle syndrome-extraction circuits}, establishes it for every number of rounds from two up, from checkable certificates and hand proofs whose finite hypotheses are machine-checked. For the single observable here a lower bound was not achieved: the flat relaxation of the distance problem gives only \DistFlatBound, and tree closure at the tight threshold did not finish within \DistNodeBudget\ nodes. Certifying distance lower bounds is where branch-and-bound is expected to be expensive; machine-checked code distances are the object of Ehatamm \textit{et al.}~\cite{ehatamm2026end}, and nothing else in this paper depends on a distance claim.

\subsection{Development data and mechanism validation}

Before the held-out campaign, development datasets over the same code covered the same noise families; \DevExactFlat\ flat certificates there were all re-verified by the reference checker, and every one of the \DevResidues\ shots the flat relaxation left open was closed by a tree, all \ReplayDevTrees\ of which are now stored and replayed (four, on the circuit dataset, were first stored after the fact, bound to the answers the development records already held). The held-out campaign supersedes those rates. Each mechanism was first validated against exact ground truth on random codes: on \EnsLadderCodes\ codes the base relaxation was tight on \EnsBaseTight, redundant-parity cuts raised that to \EnsCutsTight, and no bound ever exceeded a feasible answer's weight (\EnsImpossible\ cases). On stress-noise samples the fractional residue was \RpcResidueSix\ shots of \RpcShots\ after 6 rounds of cut generation and \RpcResidueTwentyFive\ after 25, a measured plateau whose structure we leave open.

\subsection{A strong baseline, and what the configuration explains}
\label{sec:strong}

The comparisons above use one BP-OSD configuration. We therefore reran \StrongShots\ shared nontrivial syndromes per noise level under three configurations: A, the stated one; B, min-sum with 100 iterations and OSD-CS of order 10; and C, product-sum with 100 iterations and OSD-CS of order 10. A and B use the \texttt{BpOsdDecoder} class's default min-sum scaling factor of \LdpcClassAlpha. After these results were known we added, post hoc, A$_s$ and B$_s$: A and B with the scaling factor \ScaledAlpha, a value in common use with this library (it is also the default of the library's own Sinter wrapper around this decoder, \texttt{SinterBpOsdDecoder}). It was not tuned on these data, and every factor we tried below 1 gives nearly the same result. Their answers are classified by the same stored proofs and the same rule, which reproduces the published tier-1 counts of A, B and C exactly (\texttt{qldpc\_strong\_baseline\_scaled.py}, which replays from its records and the proofs alone). Every reference answer and every receipt-pipeline answer counted in this experiment carries a checked exact receipt, flat or tree; a shot without one would be reported unresolved, and none was. BP-OSD's own raw answers, also counted, carry none. Every proof is retained, \StrongProofsFlat\ flat certificates and \StrongProofsTree\ trees, content-addressed, and every decision names its proof: \texttt{tools/replay\_strong\_baseline.py} rebuilds every number of Tables~\ref{tab:strong} and~\ref{tab:instrument} from the records and proofs alone, \StrongReplayProofChecks\ proof checks over \StrongReplayDecisions\ decisions with no producer, no solver and no BP-OSD. The largest tree has \StrongTreeNodesMax\ nodes and \StrongTreeBytesMax\ serialised bytes (a median node count of \StrongTreeNodesMedianSeven\ at $p=0.07$). Two arms are compared (Table~\ref{tab:strong}). The fixed reference is independent of BP-OSD: the relaxation's certified primal where there is one, otherwise a solver candidate kept only if a branch-dual tree for it is accepted (\StrongTreesFive\ and \StrongTreesSeven\ shots at $p=0.05$ and $0.07$). The receipt pipeline is run separately under each configuration, keeping BP-OSD's answer whenever it is certified.

Three observations follow. First, the receipt pipeline's error count depends on the BP-OSD configuration even though every answer it returns is a certified minimum-weight correction: optimality does not fix the logical frame. Under configuration C the pipeline kept a different optimum from the reference on \StrongFiveOtherOptimum\ shots at $p=0.05$ and \StrongSevenOtherOptimum\ at $p=0.07$, with a different frame on \StrongFiveOtherFrame\ and \StrongSevenOtherFrame\ of them, and committed \StrongFivePipelineStrongest\ and \StrongSevenPipelineStrongest\ errors where the reference committed \StrongFiveReference\ and \StrongSevenReference. Second, the large advantages at stress noise are advantages over an unscaled min-sum decoder, not over BP-OSD as such: at $p=0.05$ configuration C commits \StrongFiveStrongest\ errors, the same as the fixed reference, and at $p=0.07$ it commits \StrongSevenStrongest, fewer than either certified arm; and scaling alone repairs A, which at $p=0.05$ commits \ScanFiveAFailLo--\ScanFiveAFailHi\ errors unscaled, whatever error rate it is told, and \StrongFiveBpScaledA\ scaled.

On this degenerate code an unscaled decoder's answers also depend on the error rate it is told, which in exact arithmetic they cannot: with a uniform prior every min-sum update, the hard decision and the OSD ordering are homogeneous in the prior log-likelihood ratio, and declared rates whose prior log-likelihood ratios are 1, 2, 4 and 8, which floating-point arithmetic scales exactly, give identical answers on all \InvShots\ shots. Other declared rates differ only in how that ratio rounds, yet the unscaled answers change with them: across the 16 declared rates we scanned, only \InvOneAnswerA\ (A) and \InvOneAnswerB\ (B) of the \InvShots\ shots at $p=0.05$ keep one answer. For A this barely moves the counts (the ranges in Table~\ref{tab:instrument}); B finds the optimum on \ScanFiveBLo--\ScanFiveBHi\ of the $p=0.05$ syndromes and \ScanSevenBLo--\ScanSevenBHi\ of the $p=0.07$ ones. B at scaling 1 is therefore not a well-defined arm. Its entries in Tables~\ref{tab:strong} and~\ref{tab:instrument} are as run, at declared rate $p$, and we draw no comparison from them, except that at $p=0.07$ B$_s$ finds more optima (\ScanSevenBs) than B does at any declared rate. Scaled, at $p=0.05$, A$_s$ and B$_s$ fail \SensScaledFlatA\ and \SensScaledFlatB\ times at every declared rate, and B fails between \SensScaledMin\ and \SensScaledMax\ times at each scaling factor we tried below 1 (\SensAlphas; \texttt{qldpc\_bposd\_scaling\_sensitivity.py}).

Much of what goes wrong at scaling 1 is exact cancellation in the soft outputs. On the $p=0.05$ syndromes BP converges on only \ScanFiveConvA\ of \StrongShots\ for A, and on \ScanFiveZeroA\ of the \StrongShots\ some soft output ends exactly at zero, which never happens scaled. Breaking those ties removes most of the difference in optimum hits, in the spirit of the random prior perturbation Poulin and Chung use to break the symmetry of degenerate errors~\cite{poulin2008iterative}. A fixed relative perturbation of the prior log-likelihood ratios by at most $10^{-6}$ keeps the exact-arithmetic invariance and leaves no exact zero. It raises A's convergence to \ScanFiveConvAj, short of the scaled decoder's \ScanFiveConvAs, and makes A's answers independent of the declared rate on all \InvShots\ shots. It raises A's optimum hits to \ScanFiveAj\ at $p=0.05$, \ScanSevenAj\ at $p=0.07$ and \ScanHoAj\ of \HoPostStressN\ on the held-out stress syndromes, at least \ScanJitterRecoveredMinPct\% of the way from the unscaled to the scaled decoder (\texttt{qldpc\_minsum\_rate\_scan.py}, \texttt{qldpc\_minsum\_rate\_invariance.py}; both post hoc). Minimum weight is not maximum likelihood over degenerate cosets, and these counts are samples, not a performance floor.

Third, what survives is an instrument, and it needs two numbers kept apart (Table~\ref{tab:instrument}). The \emph{tier-1 rate} is how often the flat-certification route proves BP-OSD's own answer optimal: from \StrongFiveTierOneStated\% under A to \StrongFiveTierOneStrongest\% under C at $p=0.05$, and from \StrongSevenTierOneStated\% to \StrongSevenTierOneStrongest\% at $p=0.07$. A tier-1 refusal is not evidence that BP-OSD missed the optimum. In this experiment the producer issues tier 1 only on shots whose relaxation has an integral primal, those with a stored flat proof, and on every other shot BP-OSD's answer is decided by the shot's tree. Under configuration C at $p=0.07$, \StrongSevenOptHitNotTierOne\ optimal BP-OSD answers lie on such shots; on \StrongSevenRootDualC\ of them the tree is a single dual that accepts BP-OSD's own answer under the exact checker, and the remaining \StrongSevenBranchedC\ were closed by branching trees. Under the tier-1 rule as stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:ladder}, the tier-1 counts at $p=0.07$ would therefore be at least \StrongSevenTierOneRuleC\ for C (\StrongSevenTierOneRuleRateC\%), \StrongSevenTierOneRuleB\ for B as run, and \StrongSevenTierOneRuleAs\ for A$_s$ and B$_s$; at $p=0.05$ C's would be at least \StrongFiveTierOneRuleC. Because every shot carries a certified optimum $U_j$, a stronger measurement is available without the true error: BP-OSD found an optimum exactly when its answer is feasible with weight $U_j$. That \emph{optimum-hit rate} is \StrongFiveOptHitStated\% under A and \StrongFiveOptHitStrongest\% under C at $p=0.05$, and \StrongSevenOptHitStated\% and \StrongSevenOptHitStrongest\% at $p=0.07$; scaled, A$_s$ reaches \StrongFiveOptHitScaledA\% and \StrongSevenOptHitScaledA\%. Every tier-1 answer is an optimum hit; the difference between the two columns is the producer's certification cost, not the decoder's, so the optimum-hit rate, not the tier-1 rate, is the measure of decoder quality.

\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph*{Every shot is simulated.} No device has run the [[144,12,12]] code, so every rate is a property of the code, the noise model and the decoder configuration. Soundness does not depend on where a syndrome came from: the checker validates $(H,w,s)$ alone, and a syndrome from a device is the same object. What hardware would change is the certification rate.

\paragraph*{Conformance, not maximum likelihood.} A receipt proves minimum weight under the declared objective. It does not prove that the sampled fault was recovered, that the optimum is unique, or that all optima share a logical frame. Frame determinacy can itself be proven with the tree machinery, one tree per logical bit on the system augmented by that bit: on \FrameValInstances\ random instances this gave \FrameValProofs\ proofs, all correct, \FrameValRefusals\ refusals and \FrameValFalse\ false proofs, and \FrameAllCertified\ of \FrameShots\ demonstration shots of the code were proven determinate on all 12 logical bits in \FrameSecLo--\FrameSecHi\ s each. The tables above do not carry frame receipts.

\paragraph*{Other limits.} One decoder implementation; one qLDPC code; the stress comparison is not compute-matched; shot counts are limited by one workstation. Regenerating the \HoFlat\ held-out flat certificates from the shipped records gave \HoFlatByteIdentical\ byte-identical to the run of record and \HoFlatDifferentValid\ that differ while remaining exact and accepted by both checkers (\HoFlatNoHash\ circuit records carry no hash to compare); why those \HoFlatDifferentValid\ choose a different optimal dual is not established. Repeated fresh processes, a warmed process that had priced the preceding shots, and several solver thread counts all reproduce today's certificate rather than the run of record's, so none of those explains the difference; an environment difference below the pinned package versions has not been excluded. For that reason every certificate is shipped rather than left to be regenerated.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Certifying algorithms~\cite{mcconnell2011certifying} and program checking~\cite{blumkannan1995} are classical. Linear-programming decoding and the property that an integral optimum certifies maximum likelihood are due to Feldman, Wainwright and Karger~\cite{feldman2005lp}; adaptive separation to Taghavi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2008adaptive}; adaptive cut generation to Zhang and Siegel~\cite{zhangsiegel2012adaptive}. Linear-programming decoding has been extended to quantum stabilizer codes~\cite{li2018lp}; local-optimality certificates exist for Tanner codes~\cite{halabi2011local}; integer programming has been used to decode quantum codes, with optimality living inside the solver~\cite{landahl2011fault}; and linear programming has been combined with message passing and with OSD for quantum LDPC codes~\cite{javed2023low,gu2025power}. Wu \textit{et al.}~\cite{wu2025minimum} decode by a primal--dual minimum-weight parity factor algorithm that emits a proximity bound with every solution, and a dual meeting the primal's weight certifies optimality: the same form of certificate as a tier-1 receipt with $L=U$, produced inside their own decoder. Ott, Het\'enyi and Beverland~\cite{ott2025decision} give decision-tree decoders for general quantum LDPC codes, one of which is guaranteed to return a minimum-weight correction, and apply them to this code; the guarantee is the algorithm's, not a certificate a third party checks. Aghababaie Beni, Higgott and Shutty~\cite{beni2025tesseract} benchmark a search-based most-likely-error decoder on circuits of this code. Independently checkable branch-and-bound certificates are established in exact integer programming~\cite{cheung2017verifying,eifler2023computational,steffy2013valid}, so the branch-dual receipt is not new at that level of generality; what is specific here is its syndrome-decoding form, reconstructed from $(H,s)$ at every node and deployed behind a third-party heuristic decoder. Recent experiments decode with neural-network decoders fine-tuned on device data~\cite{google2025below}, and ensembling yields per-shot confidence~\cite{shutty2024efficient}; neither is an optimality proof a third party can check.

Several recent preprints are close. Krishnamoorthy \textit{et al.}~\cite{krishnamoorthy2026certified} certify decoding decisions on the same bivariate-bicycle codes; their target is the degenerate-coset maximum-likelihood class, a different objective from the minimum single-error weight certified here, established either statistically or exactly under the premises of a constant-factor partition-function estimator, with no linear program or dual, and they do not repair BP-OSD. Shen and Zhong~\cite{shen2026capability} test decoder libraries for conformance and release witnesses a standalone verifier re-derives, including a lighter feasible correction that proves a decoder's heavier answer non-minimal; their witnesses establish failures, whereas the receipts here establish optimality through an exact lower bound that excludes every smaller value on the weight lattice (an attaining dual is the special case $L=U$). Leverrier and Urbanke~\cite{leverrier2026approximating} give a decoder with circuit-level results on the same code that carries no certificate once pruned, and Sahay, Williamson and Brown~\cite{sahay2026matching} construct matching-based decoders for bivariate-bicycle codes. What is specific here is the combination: exact rational receipts, decided by a checker that imports no producer code and shares no arithmetic with it, wrapped around a third-party heuristic decoder rather than built into a decoder of our own, with branch-dual trees where the relaxation is fractional. We did not find a prior report of exact, independently re-checkable, minimum-weight receipt coverage for BP-OSD on this code; our search was logged but not exhaustive.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Seeds, the frozen protocol, per-shot records, the persisted instances and integer weight vectors, every flat certificate and every tree are released with the code under the Apache License 2.0 at \url{https://github.com/coherence-energy-labs/oneq-certificates} (release \ReleaseTag), with the companion manuscripts. The post-hoc analyses are released with their scripts: the scaled strong-baseline arms replay from their records and the stored proofs, the held-out re-analysis regenerates its faults from the seed rule and refuses to report unless it first reproduces every stored BP-OSD answer and recorded outcome, and the one-node-tree count (\texttt{qldpc\_root\_dual\_classes.py}) re-decides each single dual with the production exact checker. One command replays the complete receipt corpus against its pinned manifest with both checkers, no solver and no producer. Receipt replay is complete from the release alone. Re-running the sampling, decoding and production needs the code of the freeze commit: release v2026.09.16 of the same repository carries a snapshot of that revision, the producing scripts and every module they import, pinned by commit and sha256. The LP and MIP producers use SciPy 1.17.0 (HiGHS), BP-OSD is from \texttt{ldpc} 2.4.1, and sampling uses Stim 1.16.0. Every measured number in this paper is typeset from a macro generated from a JSON artifact, and a gate in the repository traces every number in the source, macro or literal, to the evidence tree before the manuscript builds.

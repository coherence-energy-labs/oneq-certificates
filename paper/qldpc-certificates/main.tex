\title{Exact, independently checkable optimality receipts for BP-OSD on the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code}

\OwnerAuthor
\OwnerAffiliation

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
General quantum decoding is NP-hard, which does not rule out checkable optimality witnesses on the instances that arise. We attach to each syndrome decoded by BP-OSD a dual solution of the Feldman--Wainwright--Karger linear-programming relaxation adapted to syndromes, verified in exact rational arithmetic from the check matrix, the weights and the syndrome alone, with no solver; when the relaxation is not tight, a tree of such duals over a partition of the feasible set closes the proof. On the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code, in a preregistered campaign over \HoRungs\ datasets from code-capacity to circuit-level noise, all \HoNontrivial\ nontrivial syndromes received an exact receipt, \RtHoSingleDual\ of them a single dual and \RtHoBranching\ a branching tree; all \ReplayReceipts\ stored receipts replay against a pinned manifest, each accepted by two separately written checkers. A receipt fixes the optimum, so it scores a decoder without the true error; an exact solver trusted to optimality would give the same scores, and the receipt makes them checkable. In a second preregistered campaign, \PrShots\ fresh syndromes of the same datasets were decoded by BP-OSD at the min-sum scaling factor 1 of the ldpc library's decoder class and at \ScaledAlpha, the default of its own Sinter wrapper. Scaling found significantly more optima on \PrSigMore\ datasets, each surviving a Bonferroni correction (not preregistered) over all \PrDatasets\ exact McNemar tests, for example \PrStressHitScaled\ against \PrStressHitUnscaled\ of \PrStressN\ stress syndromes and \PrCircuitHitScaled\ against \PrCircuitHitUnscaled\ of \PrCircuitN\ at circuit level; on the other \PrNoDiff\ no difference was detected at this sample size (hit counts \PrNoDiffGapMin\ to \PrNoDiffGapMax\ apart, McNemar p at least \PrNoDiffMinP). Receipts certify objective optimality only; tied optima can carry different logical frames. All results are simulations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Decoding a stabilizer code is NP-hard in general~\cite{berlekamp1978,hsieh2011np}, and belief propagation with ordered-statistics post-processing (BP-OSD)~\cite{panteleev2021degenerate,roffe2020decoding} is a standard heuristic for quantum low-density parity-check codes, including the bivariate-bicycle codes proposed as near-term memories~\cite{bravyi2024high}. A BP-OSD implementation returns a correction without a witness of its quality, and the only property routinely checked, that the correction explains the syndrome, is satisfied equally by an optimal and a suboptimal answer.

Hardness does not preclude witnesses on particular instances. A companion manuscript, \textit{Certifying quantum error-correction decoders}, released with this one (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}), attached linear-programming duals to matching decoders; those receipts proved optimality to within a small additive slack under floating-point weights. This paper asks whether the same discipline survives the move to a decoding objective that is not treated here as a single graphlike $T$-join problem (matching-based decoders for bivariate-bicycle codes do exist~\cite{sahay2026matching}; they solve a different problem than the one certified here), and demands a stronger receipt: an exact rational proof under a published integer objective.

We make three contributions. First, a receipt system for BP-OSD built from the Feldman--Wainwright--Karger (FWK) relaxation~\cite{feldman2005lp}, checked in exact arithmetic by a checker that imports no producer code, over a written input domain that all seven of its implementations (Table~\ref{tab:checkers}) enforce, with a certified repair when BP-OSD is refused and branch-dual trees when the relaxation is not tight. Second, a preregistered, frozen measurement of receipt coverage on the [[144,12,12]] code, from code-capacity to circuit-level noise under the code's published syndrome-extraction schedule. Third, the receipts as an instrument: because a receipt fixes the optimum, it scores a decoder's answers without the true error. Any exact solver run to proven optimality gives the same scores; what a receipt adds is that the optimum, and so every score, can be checked by a third party in exact arithmetic without trusting the solver. Certified per-shot benchmarking of decoders on this code is also the aim of concurrent work, for another objective (the outcome of non-degenerate maximum-likelihood decoding, judged against the sampled error) and with lower bounds that rest on a solver's floating-point arithmetic~\cite{banfi2026certified} (Sec.~\ref{sec:related}). With it, a second preregistered campaign compares two settings of one BP-OSD parameter on fresh syndromes: one setting finds significantly more optima on half of the datasets, and on the other half no difference was detected at the campaign's sample size. Every number is a simulation; Sec.~\ref{sec:scope} states what that does and does not limit.

\section{Problem and relaxation}
\label{sec:problem}

Given a sparse check matrix $H$ over $\GF$ with rows (checks) $j$ of support $N(j)$, non-negative rational weights $w$, and a syndrome $s$, the decoding problem is to find $e\in\{0,1\}^n$ minimising $w^\top e$ subject to $He=s \pmod 2$. With $w_i=1$ this is minimum Hamming weight. For heterogeneous and circuit-level noise we publish an integer weight vector, generated as $c_i=\lfloor 1000\log((1-p_i)/p_i)+\tfrac12\rfloor$, and that vector, not the logarithms it approximates, is the normative objective. On the \ScaleTreesShots\ syndromes of the development heterogeneous dataset, the scale factors $10^3$, $10^4$ and $10^5$ give exact receipts on \ScaleTreesCertified\ shots, with \ScaleTreesSupportChanges\ changes in any selected correction; one shot at every scale needs a branch-dual tree. An earlier run of this check counted only flat receipts and left that shot to the solver's reported status, under which the shot changed tier at $10^5$. Minimum weight under independent mechanism weights is not maximum likelihood over degenerate cosets, and every claim below is conformance to this stated specification.

\paragraph*{Relaxation.} Each check $j$ is relaxed to its local codeword polytope~\cite{feldman2005lp}, adapted to the syndrome bit: for every $F\subseteq N(j)$ with $|F|\not\equiv s_j \pmod 2$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in F}(1-x_i)+\sum_{i\in N(j)\setminus F}x_i\;\ge\;1 .
\label{eq:facet}
\end{equation}
Minimising $w^\top x$ over these constraints and $[0,1]^n$ lower-bounds the integer optimum, so any feasible dual solution certifies a lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[safe dual]
Write the validated facets as $a_r^\top x\ge b_r$. For any $y\ge0$, let $z_i=\max\bigl(0,(A^\top y)_i-w_i\bigr)$. Then $L=\sum_r b_r y_r-\sum_i z_i$ is a lower bound on $w^\top e$ for every feasible $e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $x\in[0,1]^n$ satisfying the facets, $w^\top x\ge (A^\top y)^\top x-z^\top x\ge y^\top Ax-\sum_i z_i\ge b^\top y-\sum_i z_i$.
\end{proof}
This is the safe bound of Neumaier and Shcherbina~\cite{neumaier2004safe} for a linear program over bounded variables, here the box $[0,1]^n$: $b^\top y$ less the positive part of the residual $A^\top y-w$, weighted by the variables' upper bounds. They evaluate it with directed rounding, so that an approximate dual from a floating-point solver still gives a valid bound; the checker evaluates it in exact rationals. The lemma holds for every $y\ge 0$, so the checker needs no feasibility tolerance: it recomputes $z$ and $L$ exactly from whatever multipliers the certificate lists. If all weights lie in $(1/D)\mathbb{Z}$, every feasible objective lies on that lattice, and a lower bound $L>U-1/D$ proves a feasible candidate of objective $U$ optimal; $L=U$ is the special case of an attaining dual, and $L>U$ is an instrument failure, refused as one.

\section{The receipt ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

A \emph{receipt} is what a checker accepts or refuses for one syndrome: the instance $(H,w,s)$, bound by hash, the candidate correction it proves optimal, if any, and a \emph{certificate}, the proof itself (none is needed for $s=0$): one dual of the relaxation, a flat certificate, or a branch-dual tree. A flat receipt is one whose certificate is flat. Every shot receives a receipt, and its tier is read from what the receipt proves.
\begin{description}
\item[Tier 0.] For $s=0$ the empty correction is optimal at cost zero, because the checker refuses negative weights. This certifies the objective, not the absence of an undetected logical fault. Trivial shots are excluded from the nontrivial rates below.
\item[Tier 1, BP-OSD's answer certified.] One dual of the shot's relaxation proves BP-OSD's own answer optimal: the checker verifies $He=s$, reconstructs every facet and cut, computes $L$ exactly and verifies $L>U-1/D$ for the answer's objective $U$.
\item[Tier 2, certified repair.] One dual proves another correction optimal, and BP-OSD's answer is heavier or is not a correction; the replacement is the relaxation's integral primal or a solver candidate, and the checker re-verifies $He=s$ from scratch. In the instance that exposed this tier, BP-OSD returned weight \DiscBpWeight\ against a tight bound of \DiscBound; the difference was a weight-\DiscFaultWeight\ logical operator, and the certified repair lay in the same coset as the sampled fault.
\item[Branching.] When no single dual closes, a mixed-integer solver proposes a candidate and the receipt is a tree: branching on a coordinate partitions the binary feasible set into $x_k=0$ and $x_k=1$, each again a syndrome instance, and every leaf carries either a safe dual proving the bound on the reduced instance or a $\GF$ row combination showing that no completion exists. The checker walks the tree in exact arithmetic without a solver; nothing the solver reports is trusted. A branching tree proves the optimum's value, so a BP-OSD answer of that value is proven optimal by it too.
\end{description}
Redundant parity checks, sums of rows over $\GF$, are generated adaptively in the manner of Zhang and Siegel~\cite{zhangsiegel2012adaptive}. Each cut is named by the rows it sums, and the checker re-derives its support and parity from $(H,s)$.

The campaign's producer assigned tiers by its own route, not by this rule. It tried a single dual through a floating-point screen whose acceptance is exact equality, $L=U$, and stored every single dual that crossed only the lattice level as a one-node ``tree''. As a result every one of the \RtHoSingleAttaining\ stored flat receipts attains $L=U$ exactly, and the lattice rule decides only the \RtHoSingleLattice\ single duals the producer filed as trees and the leaves of branching trees. Tables~\ref{tab:heldout} and~\ref{tab:instrument} count tiers by the rule above, re-decided from the stored proofs by the production exact checker (\texttt{qldpc\_tier\_by\_receipt.py}); the producer's labels are kept in Appendix~\ref{app:log}.

Two soundness statements are kept separate. The mathematical one: for inputs in the declared domain (Sec.~\ref{sec:checker}), checker acceptance implies objective optimality, by the lemma and the partition argument. The implementation one: the checker is separately reimplemented, mutation-tested, and exercised on the released corpus. The second is strong evidence, not formal verification.

\section{The checker}
\label{sec:checker}

The exact checker admits only rationals, rejecting floating-point values by type, and imports nothing beyond the Python standard library's \texttt{fractions}, \texttt{math}, \texttt{operator} and \texttt{dataclasses}; producers may use anything. The same module also carries a floating-point \emph{screen} that the producer consults before spending an exactification on a candidate; no receipt is accepted on the screen's verdict, and every count in this paper is the exact checker's. Verification costs $O(\mathrm{nnz}(H_R)+\mathrm{nnz}(C)+n)$ arithmetic operations for the reconstructed redundant rows $H_R$ and listed facet--multiplier pairs $C$, with bit complexity governed by the size of the rationals. Held-out certificates listed at most \HoMaxFacets\ facets, with numerators of at most \HoMaxNumBits\ bits and denominators of at most \HoMaxDenBits\ bits. Median exact checking time per shot ranged from \TimeExactCheckMedLo\ to \TimeExactCheckMedHi\ ms across datasets, against median producer times of \TimeLpMedLo\ to \TimeLpMedHi\ ms.

\paragraph*{The input domain.} Soundness is a statement about inputs in a declared domain, and the domain is written down (\nolinkurl{docs/QLDPC_RECEIPT_INPUT_DOMAIN.md}): every index, check entry, branch variable and facet set is a non-boolean integer in range and every listed set is free of repeats; every syndrome entry is exactly 0 or 1; a combination of checks names at least two distinct rows; multipliers are non-negative rationals, written in JSON as integer numerator and denominator in lowest terms; every listed facet is validated, including one whose multiplier is 0, which adds nothing to the bound but is still part of the receipt; a root bound above the candidate's weight is refused. An integer is written as a JSON integer literal; an integer slot holding \texttt{1.0} is refused by every implementation, the JavaScript ones by reading the token itself, since a JavaScript number cannot tell it from \texttt{1}. All seven implementations reach one verdict on each of \DomainVectors\ shared vectors, which a test runs against all of them; how the contract came to be written is in Appendix~\ref{app:log}.

\paragraph*{Separate implementations.} Table~\ref{tab:checkers} lists the seven. A reference checker for flat certificates was written from the mathematical specification of a draft of this paper, during an AI-assisted adversarial review of that draft that had no access to the repository, so without importing or inspecting the producer or the production checker; it is pinned by hash. A second tree checker was written in the project from the tree schema and the manuscript's branching tier, without importing or consulting the production checker. Third checkers, in JavaScript with exact rational arithmetic and no dependencies, were written from the manuscript's statement of the problem, in the same project, by the same author with AI assistance; the work that wrote them also wrote the harness that drives the production checker beside them. All of these are separately written, not independent: every one was written with AI assistance at one author's request, and agreement among them establishes agreement on the tested inputs, not the absence of a shared misreading of the specification. The JavaScript checkers accept all \JsFlatAccepted\ flat receipts and refuse all \JsDefects\ planted defects in \JsDefectClasses\ classes, each for its own reason, and accept all \JsTreeAccepted\ stored trees and refuse all \JsTreeDefects\ planted tree defects in \JsTreeDefectClasses\ classes.

\paragraph*{Mutation testing.} The checker's source is exercised by a hand-chosen set of targeted mutants, each of which a named test must detect: a sign inversion, a deleted feasibility pass, a syndrome taken on faith on each path that recomputes it, a forgotten parity on redundant checks, a loosened facet-parity rule, a removed slack derivation, a duplicate facet, and a lattice rule loosened by one level. Standard mutation operators applied at every eligible site of the checker module give \MutQRun\ mutants; the tests kill \MutQKilled, and each of the remaining \MutQEquivalent\ computes the same function as the original, for a reason recorded with the score. Certificate-level forgeries (a certificate replayed from another syndrome or matrix, a heavier feasible candidate, a floating-point multiplier, a dual inflated until its overload no longer proves the bound, an incomplete partition) are refused, and the second tree checker refuses all \TreeMutants\ of its forged-tree classes.

\paragraph*{Bound-only results.} The tree checker also runs without a candidate, proving that every solution of a system weighs more than a threshold $T$; frame determinacy (Sec.~\ref{sec:scope}) and the distance bound of Sec.~\ref{sec:circuit} use it. Its acceptance reports \texttt{LOWER\_BOUND\_PROVEN} with $T$ and the lattice-rounded bound $(\lfloor TD\rfloor+1)/D$, and no primal value or optimality claim.

\section{Adaptive facet separation at circuit-level degrees}
\label{sec:separation}

Adaptive linear-programming decoding, which starts with no facets and repeatedly adds violated ones, is due to Taghavi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2008adaptive}, who proved that at any point each check has at most one violated facet and that once none is violated the adaptive solution solves the full relaxation; they found the violated facet by sorting the check's coordinates. The rule used here needs no sorting and costs $O(\deg)$ per check: it is Algorithm~2 of Taghavi, Shokrollahi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2011efficient}, who note that Wadayama~\cite{wadayama2010interior} independently proposed an equivalent test of membership in the relaxation. We restate it for syndromes. For a check $j$, write $\mathrm{LHS}(F)$ for the left side of~\eqref{eq:facet} and $F_0=\{i\in N(j):x_i>\tfrac12\}$; for every $F\subseteq N(j)$,
\[
\mathrm{LHS}(F)=\mathrm{LHS}(F_0)+\sum_{i\in F\triangle F_0}\bigl|2x_i-1\bigr| ,
\]
so the minimiser over the wrong-parity family is $F_0$ itself when $|F_0|$ already has the wrong parity, and otherwise $F_0$ with the coordinate of $N(j)$ nearest $\tfrac12$ toggled. The shipped rule matches brute force over all wrong-parity subsets on \SepInstances\ random instances with \SepSuboptimal\ suboptimal separations (largest discrepancy \SepWorstGap), and agrees with a separately written reference implementation on \SepRefInstances\ further instances, including exact ties at $\tfrac12$. At convergence no facet of the full family is violated, so the lazy relaxation equals full enumeration; on \EnsLazyCodes\ random codes where enumeration was feasible the two agreed to within \EnsLazyWorst.

This matters at circuit level, where detector degrees reach \SurfaceDegreeMax\ and a full facet family has $2^{46}$ rows. Degree-capped enumeration remains sound, since a subset of facets only enlarges the feasible region, and matched full tightness on \EnsCappedTight\ random codes; adaptive separation needs no cap. Deployed first, with enumeration only on refusal, it gave identical coverage on a circuit-level surface-code benchmark at \TieredMs\ against \EnumMs\ ms per shot, a factor of \TieredSpeedup.

\subsection{Circuit level under the published schedule}
\label{sec:circuit}

The circuit dataset implements the code's published depth-8 syndrome-extraction cycle~\cite{bravyi2024high} in Stim, memory-$Z$ over three rounds at uniform circuit-level noise $p=0.002$, with 288 detectors and 2{,}304 mechanisms under published integer weights. The noise family is ours and, unlike the original error model, does not fault idle locations. At three rounds this leaves the set of distinct fault signatures unchanged, which is what the distance comparison below needs: aligned by the measurement events each detector compares, the model's \EqMechanisms\ mechanisms (distinct detector and observable signatures) are exactly the \EqMechanismsPthree\ of the companion manuscript's model of the same cycle, whose noise does include idle locations, projected to its $Z$-type detectors (\texttt{qldpc\_refsched\_p3\_equality.py}); the idle faults add no signature. Equal signature sets do not establish equal mechanism probabilities, likelihood weights or logical error rates for the two models, and no result here compares them. The transcription was validated at the fault level, not only noiselessly: an operation generator written separately from a hashed layer manifest reproduces the circuit exactly (\XvalOps\ operations, \XvalRecords\ measurement records), and every single $X$ and $Z$ fault at every location, \XvalFaults\ in total, was propagated by a separately written symplectic engine and compared with Stim's detector and observable signature, with \XvalMismatches\ mismatches. The held-out run certified all \HoRefschedNontrivial\ nontrivial shots. The circuit declares one logical observable, and it agreed with the sampled truth on \HoRefschedObsAgree\ of \HoRefschedNontrivial\ shots; this is a result for that observable, not an all-12-logical-qubit block error, and zero observed disagreements in \HoRefschedNontrivial\ shots bounds the underlying disagreement rate below $1-0.05^{1/149}\le\HoRefschedObsUpperPct\%$ (one-sided 95\%). BP-OSD's own answers, scored the same way, also agreed on \HoRefschedBposdObsAgree\ of \HoRefschedNontrivial: the receipts replaced \HoRefschedReplaced\ of them with a strictly lighter correction, and all \HoRefschedReplacedSameObs\ replacements lie in the same logical class as the answer they replaced, so at this noise level the receipt changed the weight of the answer and never its outcome (\texttt{tools/bposd\_circuit\_outcome.py}).

Two further circuit datasets, a surface-code control and a custom sequential schedule, were checked only in floating point. Floating-point acceptance is not a receipt in the sense of this paper, and they are not counted here.

\paragraph*{The distance of the declared observable.} Let $d_{\mathrm{DEM}}$ be the weight of the lightest undetectable fault that flips the circuit dataset's declared observable. A \EqWitnessWeight-column fault in the persisted instance, the companion manuscript's two-round witness shifted by one round, fires no detector and flips the observable, and no nine of its columns do both, so $d_{\mathrm{DEM}}\le\EqWitnessWeight$; Stim's heuristic search had found only weight \DistUpper. Because the mechanism sets are equal, and the declared observable is, mechanism by mechanism, the first of the companion model's twelve, so that a fault flipping it flips one of them, the companion manuscript's certified lower bound for this cycle at three rounds applies to this model unchanged; over its twelve observables it is 10, so $d_{\mathrm{DEM}}=10$ for the declared observable at three rounds. That is derived from the companion's certificate and its time-shift theorem; only three rounds were compared. The companion, \emph{Machine-checkable lower-bound certificates for the circuit-level distances of bivariate-bicycle syndrome-extraction circuits}, establishes circuit distance 10 for every number of rounds from two up under its own fault placement. Each under its own noise model, the same value was found by an exhaustive search for twelve noisy rounds~\cite{crocker2026gross} and by exact enumeration for up to three rounds~\cite{pan2026interleaving}, neither of which records a certificate. Guancheng Pan's later research records certify the lower bound with VeriPB proofs for up to twelve rounds~\cite{pan2026e037} and argue it for every number of rounds: under SD6 circuit noise from solver runs without certificates~\cite{pan2026e043}, and for his re-implementation of IBM's fault model from those certificates and a written argument without a certificate for the step~\cite{pan2026e045}. Bin Cheng, Naixu Guo and Feng Pan~\cite{cheng2026certifying} certify 10 in both bases at fixed numbers of rounds (one noisy cycle of the memory instruction of IBM's architecture study, twelve noisy cycles of the circuits of IBM's public code) with an exact solver whose completeness they prove, pairing its lower bound with a logical error of matching weight; their records carry no proof object for the lower bound. This paper's own machinery did not reach the bound: the flat relaxation of the distance problem gives only \DistFlatBound, and tree closure at the tight threshold did not finish within \DistNodeBudget\ nodes. Certifying distance lower bounds is where branch-and-bound is expected to be expensive; machine-checked code distances are the object of Ehatamm \textit{et al.}~\cite{ehatamm2026end}, and nothing else in this paper depends on a distance claim.

\subsection{A strong baseline, and what the configuration explains}
\label{sec:strong}

We also reran \StrongShots\ shared nontrivial syndromes per noise level under three configurations: A, the campaign's; B, min-sum with 100 iterations and OSD-CS of order 10; and C, product-sum with 100 iterations and OSD-CS of order 10. A and B use the class default scaling factor of \LdpcClassAlpha. After these results were known we added, post hoc, A$_s$ and B$_s$: A and B with the scaling factor \ScaledAlpha. It was not tuned on these data, and every factor we tried below 1 gives nearly the same result. Their answers are classified by the same stored proofs and the same rule (\texttt{qldpc\_strong\_baseline\_scaled.py}, which replays from its records and the proofs alone). Every reference answer and every receipt-pipeline answer counted in this experiment carries a checked exact receipt, flat or tree; a shot without one would be reported unresolved, and none was. BP-OSD's own raw answers, also counted, carry none. Every proof is retained, \StrongProofsFlat\ flat certificates and \StrongProofsTree\ trees, content-addressed, and every decision names its proof: \texttt{tools/replay\_strong\_baseline.py} rebuilds every number of Tables~\ref{tab:strong} and~\ref{tab:instrument} from the records and proofs alone, \StrongReplayProofChecks\ proof checks over \StrongReplayDecisions\ decisions with no producer, no solver and no BP-OSD. A check is one proof re-decided for one answer: a flat certificate is the syndrome's dual and proves every answer of the optimal weight, so a proof shared by two different answers is checked for each, and decisions sharing both a proof and an answer are checked once. The largest tree has \StrongTreeNodesMax\ nodes and \StrongTreeBytesMax\ serialised bytes (a median node count of \StrongTreeNodesMedianSeven\ at $p=0.07$). The fixed reference is independent of BP-OSD: the relaxation's certified primal where there is one, otherwise a solver candidate kept only if a branch-dual tree for it is accepted (\StrongTreesFive\ and \StrongTreesSeven\ shots at $p=0.05$ and $0.07$). The receipt pipeline is run separately under each configuration, keeping BP-OSD's answer whenever it is certified.

Three observations follow. First, the receipt pipeline's error count depends on the BP-OSD configuration even though every answer it returns is a certified minimum-weight correction: optimality does not fix the logical frame. Under configuration C the pipeline kept a different optimum from the reference on \StrongFiveOtherOptimum\ shots at $p=0.05$ and \StrongSevenOtherOptimum\ at $p=0.07$, with a different frame on \StrongFiveOtherFrame\ and \StrongSevenOtherFrame\ of them, and committed \StrongFivePipelineStrongest\ and \StrongSevenPipelineStrongest\ errors where the reference committed \StrongFiveReference\ and \StrongSevenReference. Second, the large advantages at stress noise are advantages over an unscaled min-sum decoder, not over BP-OSD as such: at $p=0.05$ configuration C commits \StrongFiveStrongest\ errors, the same as the fixed reference, and at $p=0.07$ it commits \StrongSevenStrongest, fewer than either certified arm; and scaling alone repairs A, which at $p=0.05$ commits \ScanFiveAFailLo--\ScanFiveAFailHi\ errors unscaled, whatever error rate it is told, and \StrongFiveBpScaledA\ scaled.

On this degenerate code an unscaled decoder's answers also depend on the error rate it is told, which in exact arithmetic they cannot: with a uniform prior every min-sum update, the hard decision and the OSD ordering are homogeneous in the prior log-likelihood ratio, and declared rates whose prior log-likelihood ratios are 1, 2, 4 and 8, which floating-point arithmetic scales exactly, give identical answers on all \InvShots\ shots. Other declared rates differ only in how that ratio rounds, yet the unscaled answers change with them: across the 16 declared rates we scanned, only \InvOneAnswerA\ (A) and \InvOneAnswerB\ (B) of the \InvShots\ shots at $p=0.05$ keep one answer. For A this barely moves the counts (the ranges in Table~\ref{tab:instrument}); B finds the optimum on \ScanFiveBLo--\ScanFiveBHi\ of the $p=0.05$ syndromes and \ScanSevenBLo--\ScanSevenBHi\ of the $p=0.07$ ones. B at scaling 1 is therefore not a well-defined arm. Its entries in Tables~\ref{tab:strong} and~\ref{tab:instrument} are as run, at declared rate $p$, and we draw no comparison from them, except that at $p=0.07$ B$_s$ finds more optima (\ScanSevenBs) than B does at any declared rate. Scaled, at $p=0.05$, A$_s$ and B$_s$ fail \SensScaledFlatA\ and \SensScaledFlatB\ times at every declared rate, and B fails between \SensScaledMin\ and \SensScaledMax\ times at each scaling factor we tried below 1 (\SensAlphas; \texttt{qldpc\_bposd\_scaling\_sensitivity.py}).

Much of what goes wrong at scaling 1 is exact cancellation in the soft outputs. On the $p=0.05$ syndromes BP converges on only \ScanFiveConvA\ of \StrongShots\ for A, and on \ScanFiveZeroA\ of the \StrongShots\ some soft output ends exactly at zero, which never happens scaled. Breaking those ties removes most of the difference in optimum hits, in the spirit of the random prior perturbation Poulin and Chung use to break the symmetry of degenerate errors~\cite{poulin2008iterative}. Concurrently, Fregona \textit{et al.}~\cite{fregona2026dealing} prove that Tanner-graph symmetries they call reflections keep parallel product-sum BP from converging on some weight-3 errors of bivariate-bicycle codes including this one, report a numerical instability in the BP-OSD implementation of Ref.~\cite{roffe2020decoding} (infinite messages of opposite sign combining to NaN), and repair both by modifying BP, one variant with a bounded random perturbation. A fixed relative perturbation of the prior log-likelihood ratios by at most $10^{-6}$ keeps the exact-arithmetic invariance and leaves no exact zero. It raises A's convergence to \ScanFiveConvAj, short of the scaled decoder's \ScanFiveConvAs, and makes A's answers independent of the declared rate on all \InvShots\ shots. It raises A's optimum hits to \ScanFiveAj\ at $p=0.05$, \ScanSevenAj\ at $p=0.07$ and \ScanHoAj\ of \HoPostStressN\ on the held-out stress syndromes, at least \ScanJitterRecoveredMinPct\% of the way from the unscaled to the scaled decoder (\texttt{qldpc\_minsum\_rate\_scan.py}, \texttt{qldpc\_minsum\_rate\_invariance.py}; both post hoc). Minimum weight is not maximum likelihood over degenerate cosets, and these counts are samples, not a performance floor.

Third, what survives is an instrument, and it needs two numbers kept apart (Table~\ref{tab:instrument}). The \emph{tier-1 count} is how often one dual proves BP-OSD's own answer optimal: from \RtFiveTierOneA\ under A to \RtFiveTierOneC\ under C of \StrongShots\ at $p=0.05$, and from \RtSevenTierOneA\ to \RtSevenTierOneC\ at $p=0.07$. Because every shot carries a certified optimum $U_j$, a stronger measurement is available without the true error: BP-OSD found an optimum exactly when its answer is feasible with weight $U_j$, whether a single dual or a branching tree proves $U_j$. That \emph{optimum-hit rate} is \StrongFiveOptHitStated\% under A and \StrongFiveOptHitStrongest\% under C at $p=0.05$, and \StrongSevenOptHitStated\% and \StrongSevenOptHitStrongest\% at $p=0.07$; scaled, A$_s$ reaches \StrongFiveOptHitScaledA\% and \StrongSevenOptHitScaledA\%. The difference between the two columns is the optimal answers whose proof needs branching, a property of the relaxation on that syndrome rather than of the decoder, so the optimum-hit rate is the measure of decoder quality.

Before the held-out campaign, development datasets over the same code covered the same noise families. Their \DevExactFlat\ flat certificates were checked by the reference checker when produced and were not stored, so that check cannot be replayed. A later re-run of the same producer reproduced every development per-shot record except the certificate's hash, which differed on \DevExportDrift\ shots (another dual proving the same answer optimal); its \DevExportCerts\ certificates are stored (\nolinkurl{evidence/p2_certificates.jsonl.gz}), \DevExportSameHash\ of them with the hash the original run recorded, and a checker written in Lean, whose acceptance is proven there to imply optimality (the proof covers the checking function, not its JSON reader or the compiler), accepts all \DevLeanAccepted\ and refuses all \DevLeanForgeries\ forgeries made from them (\texttt{tools/check\_lean\_certificate.py}, which needs Lean and Mathlib). \section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph*{Every shot is simulated.} No device has run the [[144,12,12]] code, so every rate is a property of the code, the noise model and the decoder configuration. Soundness does not depend on where a syndrome came from: the checker validates $(H,w,s)$ alone, and a syndrome from a device is the same object. What hardware would change is the certification rate.

\paragraph*{Conformance, not maximum likelihood.} A receipt proves minimum weight under the declared objective. It does not prove that the sampled fault was recovered, that the optimum is unique, or that all optima share a logical frame. Frame determinacy can itself be proven with the tree machinery in bound-only mode, one tree per logical bit on the system augmented by that bit: on \FrameValInstances\ random instances this gave \FrameValProofs\ proofs, all correct, \FrameValRefusals\ refusals and \FrameValFalse\ false proofs, and \FrameAllCertified\ of \FrameShots\ demonstration shots of the code were proven determinate on all 12 logical bits in \FrameSecLo--\FrameSecHi\ s each. The tables above do not carry frame receipts.

\paragraph*{Other limits.} One decoder implementation; one qLDPC code; the stress comparison is not compute-matched; shot counts are limited by one workstation. Every certificate is shipped rather than left to be regenerated, because regenerating them does not reproduce every one byte for byte (Appendix~\ref{app:log}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Certifying algorithms~\cite{mcconnell2011certifying} and program checking~\cite{blumkannan1995} are classical. Linear-programming decoding and the property that an integral optimum certifies maximum likelihood are due to Feldman, Wainwright and Karger~\cite{feldman2005lp}; adaptive linear-programming decoding to Taghavi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2008adaptive}, and the separation rule used here, which needs no sorting, to Taghavi, Shokrollahi and Siegel~\cite{taghavi2011efficient} and, independently, Wadayama~\cite{wadayama2010interior}; adaptive cut generation to Zhang and Siegel~\cite{zhangsiegel2012adaptive}. Linear-programming decoding has been extended to quantum stabilizer codes~\cite{li2018lp}; local-optimality certificates exist for Tanner codes~\cite{halabi2011local}; integer programming has been used to decode quantum codes, with optimality living inside the solver~\cite{landahl2011fault}, and such a solver run to optimality would score a decoder exactly as a receipt does, but without a proof a third party can check; for classical codes, Draper, Yedidia and Wang~\cite{draper2007ml} made adaptive linear-programming decoding a maximum-likelihood decoder by requiring the least certain bit of each fractional solution to be integral, which the branching tier here does by branching, and left the resulting mixed-integer program, and so the optimality, to an off-the-shelf solver; and linear programming has been combined with message passing and with OSD for quantum LDPC codes~\cite{javed2023low,gu2025power}. Wu \textit{et al.}~\cite{wu2025minimum} decode by a primal--dual minimum-weight parity factor algorithm that emits a proximity bound with every solution, and a dual meeting the primal's weight certifies optimality. That is the form of every stored flat receipt here, all of which attain $L=U$; the lattice rule closes only the single duals the producer stored as one-node trees and the leaves of branching trees. On classical block codes, Hwang, Won and Hwang~\cite{hwang2026certified} certify per frame, by a linear-programming dual bound or a distance test, that a neural decoder's candidate is a global maximum-likelihood solution; their dual test runs in floating point with a margin, so that a frame it certifies is ``$\varepsilon$-optimal in likelihood cost rather than exactly optimal''. What differs is where the certificate lives and who checks it: here a separately written checker re-derives every facet and cut from $(H,s)$ in exact rationals, outside the decoder that produced the answer, and a tree of duals takes over where no single dual closes. Ott, Het\'enyi and Beverland~\cite{ott2025decision} give decision-tree decoders for general quantum LDPC codes, one of which is guaranteed to return a minimum-weight correction, and apply them to this code; the guarantee is the algorithm's, not a certificate a third party checks. Aghababaie Beni, Higgott and Shutty~\cite{beni2025tesseract} benchmark a search-based most-likely-error decoder on circuits of this code. Safe dual bounds for linear programs solved in floating point go back to Neumaier and Shcherbina~\cite{neumaier2004safe}, whose bound is the lemma of Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}, and independently checkable branch-and-bound certificates are established in exact integer programming~\cite{cheung2017verifying,eifler2023computational,steffy2013valid}, so the branch-dual receipt is not new at that level of generality, and branch-and-bound over syndrome-decoding problems is the search of Ref.~\cite{banfi2026certified}; what is specific here is a checkable syndrome-decoding form, in which every node's bound is an exact dual the checker re-derives from $(H,s)$, deployed behind a third-party heuristic decoder. Recent experiments decode with neural-network decoders fine-tuned on device data~\cite{google2025below}, and ensembling yields per-shot confidence~\cite{shutty2024efficient}; neither is an optimality proof a third party can check.

Several recent preprints are close. Closest in setting is Banfi \textit{et al.}~\cite{banfi2026certified}, who certify per-shot outcomes on this code at three rounds, and on the [[72,12,6]] code, ``under the full circuit-level noise model'' of Ref.~\cite{bravyi2024high}, as ``a common reference for comparing decoders and codes''. The two methods differ on four counts. \emph{The objective.} They certify the outcome of non-degenerate maximum-likelihood decoding, whether the class of a minimum-cost pattern is the sampled error's class, by upper and lower bounds on the minimum cost in the true and in the competing classes, with costs $\log((1-q_j)/q_j)$ in nats, and they leave near-ties undecided; a receipt here fixes the minimum of a published integer objective for a syndrome, needs no sampled error, and certifies no class (Sec.~\ref{sec:scope}). \emph{Lower bounds and infeasibility.} Theirs come from disjoint-detector bounds, a charging bound whose linear program they ``solve \ldots\ with HiGHS, recheck its constraints, and scale the charges down when necessary'', best-first branch-and-bound, an adaptive parity-cut linear program of the Feldman and Taghavi--Siegel family used in Sec.~\ref{sec:separation}, and HiGHS mixed-integer dual bounds and zero-objective infeasibility tests; they add ``exhaustive tests on small instances and independent solver comparisons'', and on this code re-evaluated every certified failure with the exact integer-programming decoder bundled with Tesseract. Here every lower bound is a safe dual that the checker recomputes in exact rationals, and every infeasible leaf a $\GF$ row combination it re-derives. \emph{Floating point.} Their Appendix C.7 states that ``costs, charges, and solver bounds use floating-point arithmetic'', that ``the current results therefore rely on the solver and its tolerances for numerical lower bounds and for the infeasibility claims'', and that the margin $\varepsilon$ ``avoids verdicts near a tie, but does not bound every rounding error''; a class minimum counts as exact when the solver's dual bound and the incumbent agree to within $10^{-10}$. Here floating point enters only the rounding that publishes the integer weights (Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}), the producer and its screen, and no acceptance depends on it. \emph{What a third party receives.} They check every witness ``against the detector and logical equations using integer arithmetic modulo two'', a check that, in their words, ``establishes feasibility, not optimality or the validity of the lower bound reported by a solver''; the paper describes no proof of the lower bounds that a third party can re-check without the solver (we did not inspect their code). A receipt here is such a proof, a file that two separately written checkers accept or refuse from $(H,w,s)$ alone. Their verdict says more about logical outcomes; a receipt says less, exactly, in a form anyone can re-check; neither subsumes the other. Krishnamoorthy \textit{et al.}~\cite{krishnamoorthy2026certified} certify decoding decisions on bivariate-bicycle codes including this one; their target is the degenerate-coset maximum-likelihood class among candidates around the BP+OSD-0 answer, from which all their estimator-based decoders start, a different objective from the minimum single-error weight certified here. Their certificates are of three kinds: a paired bootstrap test on their sampler's estimates; an exact decision (their Lemma~1) when every class's partition function is estimated within a known constant factor, as the WISH estimator does with probability $1-\delta$, a route they validated with an exact optimization oracle on the distance-5 rotated surface code only (their Sec.~8.1) and whose realisation with a quantum annealer as that oracle they call ``a concrete, if currently speculative, possibility''; and deterministic bounds from mini-bucket elimination (their Sec.~7.6), which at two bucket sizes certified 37 and 38 of 40 syndromes of the [[72,12,6]] code and none on this code, at 272~s per decode (their Sec.~8.5). None uses a linear program or a dual. On the [[72,12,6]] code their sampling decoder changes eight of BP+OSD-0's decisions for the better and three for the worse, across all rates (their Sec.~8.3), by changing the logical class, not by returning a certified minimum-weight correction. Shen and Zhong~\cite{shen2026capability} test decoder libraries for conformance and release witnesses a standalone verifier re-derives, including a lighter feasible correction that proves a decoder's heavier answer non-minimal; their witnesses establish failures, whereas the receipts here establish optimality through an exact lower bound that excludes every smaller value on the weight lattice. Leverrier and Urbanke~\cite{leverrier2026approximating} give a decoder with circuit-level results on the same code that carries no certificate once pruned, and Sahay, Williamson and Brown~\cite{sahay2026matching} construct matching-based decoders for bivariate-bicycle codes. What is specific here is the combination: exact rational receipts, decided by a checker that imports no producer code and shares no arithmetic with it, wrapped around a third-party heuristic decoder rather than built into a decoder of our own, with branch-dual trees where the relaxation is fractional. We did not find a prior report of exact, independently re-checkable, minimum-weight receipt coverage for BP-OSD on this code; the nearest, Ref.~\cite{banfi2026certified}, certifies another objective with bounds that rest on a solver. Our search, repeated for this revision, was logged but not exhaustive.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Seeds, the frozen protocols of both campaigns, per-shot records, the persisted instances and integer weight vectors, every flat certificate and every tree are released with the code under the Apache License 2.0 at \url{https://github.com/coherence-energy-labs/oneq-certificates} (release \ReleaseTag), with the companion manuscripts. The post-hoc analyses are released with their scripts: the scaled strong-baseline arms replay from their records and the stored proofs, the held-out re-decode reproduces every stored BP-OSD answer before it reports, and the receipt-defined tiers are re-decided from the stored proofs with the production exact checker. One command replays the complete receipt corpus against its pinned manifest with both checkers, no solver and no producer. A second, \texttt{tools/replay\_qldpc\_scaling\_campaign.py}, replays the preregistered comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:prereg} from its archived inputs (\nolinkurl{evidence/qldpc_scaling_prereg/inputs/}): for each dataset, the persisted check matrix by hash, the integer weight vector, the decoder's settings and channel, the logical test's stabilizer rows or observable columns, and every shot's sampled fault or observable value, bound to the record files by a manifest whose hash the tool pins. It re-checks all \PrShots\ receipts with both of their checkers, recomputes every answer's objective, score and logical outcome, and every count and McNemar $p$ of Tables~\ref{tab:prereg} and~\ref{tab:prereglogical}, and refuses on any mismatch; it needs Python's standard library and the checkers' own modules, and loads no producer, solver, decoder, sampler or NumPy. The archive was written once, by calling the runner's model-building code, and is accepted only where re-drawing the campaign's seeded samplers reproduced every recorded syndrome. A replay never re-samples: Stim's API reference for \texttt{compile\_sampler} states that a seeded sampler's results are not consistent between Stim versions and may differ between machines with different SIMD widths or when the same shots are drawn in different batches, so no number here rests on re-sampling elsewhere. Receipt replay is complete from the release alone. The timed re-run of Sec.~\ref{sec:prereg} needs the producer and the private development repository's history, not the archive. The public repository does not carry that history: the freeze, results and preregistration commits of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} are in the private repository, and the public one holds the files as released. Re-running the held-out sampling, decoding and production needs the code of the freeze commit: release v2026.09.16 of the public repository carries a snapshot of that revision, the producing scripts and every module they import, pinned by commit and sha256. The LP and MIP producers use SciPy 1.17.0 (HiGHS), BP-OSD is from \texttt{ldpc} 2.4.1, and sampling uses Stim 1.16.0. Every measured number in this paper is typeset from a macro generated from a JSON artifact, and a gate in the repository traces every number in the source, macro or literal, to the evidence tree before the manuscript builds. The generator rounds so as not to say more than the evidence: point percentages and lower bounds down at the last digit shown, upper bounds up, a range outward unless its ends are entries of a table it quotes, and every other value, timings and $p$-values included, to the nearest value at the digits shown.

\paragraph*{Use of AI tools.} AI tools assisted with software development and manuscript preparation. Reviews of this manuscript by AI agents that had not written it, and external audits of its drafts and earlier releases, informed it. Of the two latest audits, one calls itself AI-assisted and the other states that it was done by an AI model with AI sub-agents; the author's records describe the review of a draft that wrote the reference flat checker (Sec.~\ref{sec:checker}) as AI-assisted; the others do not say who or what reviewed. Every review, audit and referee this paper mentions is one of these, and none is third-party peer review. The author is responsible for all content.

\paragraph*{The JavaScript checkers.} Writing them exposed no ambiguity in the specification; it exposed three rules of the harness that no stored receipt reached (no stored tree closes a leaf by infeasibility, sits on the lattice boundary or overloads a multiplier), which two deliberately broken copies of the tree checker passed until a hand-built instance whose one branch is provably infeasible was added, judged by the production checker as well. An external audit then found that both programs, having written their verdicts, exited before a piped standard output had drained, delivering 65{,}536 bytes of a larger batch with exit code 0; the harnesses caught it only because they reconcile receipt identifiers, and the programs now let the process end on its own.

\paragraph*{The floating-point screen.} One surviving mutant of the first mutation score exposed a defect in the screen, a path no receipt passes through: it admitted negative weights within its tolerance and could then prove a correction optimal while a lighter one existed. The screen now refuses every negative weight; the exact and tree checkers that decide every receipt always did.

\paragraph*{The input domain.} After the held-out campaign the checkers were changed to refuse syndrome entries other than 0 and 1, which they had reduced modulo 2. An external audit of the next release found the implementations still reading malformed input differently: the production checker truncated a check index \texttt{0.9} to \texttt{0} and accepted out-of-range check indices, the second tree checker read a syndrome entry \texttt{2} as \texttt{0}, and integer slots were coerced elsewhere. The contract of Sec.~\ref{sec:checker} replaced all of these, with root guards in the two Python tree checkers to match the JavaScript one. No stored receipt was affected, since every integer of the stored corpus is a plain in-range integer. The final referee of this revision, an AI agent, then found that six of the seven implementations skipped a facet with multiplier 0 before validating it, so that a zero-multiplier facet naming check 999, or repeating another facet, was accepted unread; every implementation now validates every listed facet first, and the shared vectors include each facet rule on a zero-multiplier facet, flat and at tree depth 1. The stored corpora do list such facets, \ZeroMultFacets\ of them in \ZeroMultReceipts\ receipts, and every one is well formed: all \ReplayReceipts\ receipts, the strong baseline's stored proofs and the \PrShots\ receipts of the second campaign verify under the changed bytes. Each change moved every pin of a changed checker with its previous value kept and its own record (\nolinkurl{evidence/qldpc_receipt_reverification_2026-11-03.json}, then \nolinkurl{evidence/qldpc_receipt_reverification_2026-11-03b.json}).

\paragraph*{Bound-only results.} Until the external audit, the bound-only mode of Sec.~\ref{sec:checker} reported an exact optimum with a primal weight derived from $T$; no number in this paper read those fields.

\paragraph*{The second campaign's deviation.} Under the committed runner the six-round redundant-parity LP took 18.6, 42.9 and 206.5 s on three of the first 81 syndromes of the phenomenological dataset at $p=0.02$, which did not finish in fifty minutes and wrote no record; a diagnosis on the same seeds printed timings only. The \PrCommittedDatasets\ datasets run before the change ran as committed; the \PrDeviatedDatasets\ run after it carry their own code hashes in the summary, and of those only the phenomenological dataset at $p=0.02$ uses the changed LP.

\paragraph*{Post-hoc analyses.} The scaled strong-baseline arms, the held-out re-decode of Table~\ref{tab:posthoc}, the declared-rate scans and the receipt-defined tier recount were all run after the results they analyse were known, and are labelled post hoc where they appear.

High-priority reporting correction: the 6.6–8.2× timing excludes checking
The timing code measures decoding with export and conversion of the exported regions into cuts. The exact checker is called after the timed interval. This is visible in the native-dual timing implementation.

Consequently, 6.6–8.2 times a plain decode is a witness-production measurement, not a complete decode–produce–verify measurement.

The number is useful. Its label needs to be precise:

Report it as decoding, exporting, and constructing the witness.
Measure exact checking separately on the same trials.
Report the complete latency if making an end-to-end certification-cost claim.
I would not add checker medians from another experiment to these medians. Different objectives, cohorts, and timing methods make that an unreliable reconstruction of the missing measurement.

The fold hypothesis is materially better
The revised theorem requires equality of the relevant propagated mid-cycle fault-image sets. That is the appropriate object for the argument.

Equal-looking raw noise locations do not by themselves ensure equal propagated effects: conjugation through different circuit layers can change those effects. The revised condition addresses that issue, including the treatment of measurement, reset, and boundary faults.

The associated regression checks reject the problematic raw-noise symmetry example while accepting the intended noise family. I did not find a counterexample to the revised argument under its stated hypotheses.

The key areas are the new campaign in Section VI C, the related-work discussion in Section VIII, and the reproducibility claims in Section IX.
Philbrick_exact-receipts-BP-OSD-144-12-12_v2026.11.05.pdf

The core receipt machinery held up
The safe dual bound, lattice closing rule, branch partition, and infeasible-leaf treatment survived the mathematical review and additional small-instance checks.

The audit also successfully replayed:

the 2,042 stored receipts through both supplied checkers;
the strong-baseline evidence covering 4,000 decisions;

The new campaign's optimum-hit counts, paired comparisons, and reported statistical calculations were recomputed from the stored records. I found no confirmed optimum-scoring defect.

The November 3 version already acknowledged the scaling artifact. The material advance in November 5 is the fresh, broader evidence and the more careful comparison with competitive configurations. The paper also reports datasets where the scaling advantage is small or reverses, which is important for avoiding a one-sided story.

This supports a better framing: exact receipts provide a way to audit decoder performance against a specified optimization objective, including well-configured decoders.

Moderate reproducibility issue: the new heterogeneous input is not fully archived
The new campaign constructs its heterogeneous integer weight vector inside the producer's build function. That exact normative vector is not persisted as a complete standalone campaign input, and the older heterogeneous fixtures are not the same vector.

The current reconstruction works: the 6,500 receipts verify after it. The remaining problem is the stronger producer-free replay claim. A verifier should not need to call the producer's model-building code to recover the precise instance whose optimum it is checking.

The concrete fix is straightforward:

Archive the exact matrices, integer weights, and logical maps for each campaign dataset.
Bind them to the records with hashes.
Provide a replay path that consumes those archived inputs directly.
The existing pinned receipt corpus already has a stronger replay story. Extend that standard to the new campaign.

A suspected circuit-outcome problem did not survive further checking
I initially investigated whether the circuit records had lost the sampled logical truth because they lacked an explicit truth-bit field.

They have one observable bit, and that bit can be recovered from the stored candidate observable and its agreement flag. Across all 500 circuit records, the independently reconstructed bit agrees across all three recorded answers.

I am not retaining missing circuit truth as an audit finding.

The seeded circuit sampling stream did not reproduce identically on this host, which is a portability issue worth documenting. Stim explicitly warns that identical seeds do not guarantee identical results across changes in machine SIMD capabilities, version, or sampling procedure. That is separate from replaying the archived outcomes. See the Stim sampling API documentation.

Close prior work needs an explicit comparison
The related-work section should address:

Banfi, Ferrari, Favano, Tarable, and Barletta, "Certified Non-Degenerate Maximum-Likelihood Benchmarking of Quantum LDPC Decoders," arXiv:2610.28008.

Its per-shot benchmarking objective and quantum-LDPC setting are sufficiently close that omission leaves an avoidable opening for a referee.

This does not erase Paper A's exact-receipt contribution. The comparison should identify precisely:

what objective each method certifies;
how lower bounds and infeasibility claims are validated;
where floating-point tolerances enter;
whether a third party receives a portable, independently replayable exact proof.
The numerical-verification discussion in that paper's Appendix C.7 is especially relevant to this distinction.

Costs need to follow the new headline experiment
The paper gives useful production and checking timings for the earlier corpus. The new 6,500-shot campaign should have its own stage-separated cost summary.

Report production and checking latency distributions, timeouts, hardware, thread counts, and resource limits. Medians alone will not show the expensive cases that matter operationally.

I would not infer a slowdown by dividing total campaign runtime by shots and comparing that with an older producer-only median. Those are different measurements. The finding is that the new campaign lacks the most useful cost breakdown, not that its earlier timing claims have been disproved.

Two smaller wording changes would improve precision:

Replace "indistinguishable" with "no difference detected at this sample size," unless an equivalence analysis is actually provided.
Qualify "At three rounds that costs nothing" when discussing omitted idle faults. Equality of distinct fault signatures supports the stated distance comparison; it does not establish equality of probabilities, likelihood weights, or logical-error rates.
My judgment on A: the evidence is stronger than the earlier "default scaling was poor" narrative suggests. It should remain a focused methods paper about exact benchmarking and receipts, with tuned baselines central to the presentation.

6. What remains of the broader ONE_Q concerns
These papers now answer more of the earlier objections. They do not yet, by themselves, establish the complete operational case you originally described.

Several concerns have concrete resolutions
The comparison with stronger decoder configurations is much better supported. Paper B directly investigates native Sparse Blossom witnesses. Its scoring correction is substantial. Paper C's large proof artifacts are accessible and hash-bound. Paper D's threshold-crossing witnesses replay.

Those are real advances, and they should be credited rather than treated as permanently unresolved suspicions.

Operational benefit still requires its own demonstration
The papers would support a stronger ONE_Q systems claim if a single frozen evaluation measured the following together:

The distinction you originally emphasized remains fundamental: a valid certificate proves a statement about its specified model and objective. Hardware adequacy is an additional empirical claim.
